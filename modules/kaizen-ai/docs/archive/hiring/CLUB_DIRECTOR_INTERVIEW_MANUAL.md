# Interview manual — Club Director (founder's copy)

> **Still in use, with one correction (2026-09-02).** The role is the **Program
> Director** ([`PROGRAM_DIRECTOR.md`](./PROGRAM_DIRECTOR.md)) — hourly, part
> time, no profit share — so ignore every reference below to a partnership,
> profit share or founding-partner offer. The question bank and the scoring
> rubric are unchanged and remain the process.

You are not hiring a résumé; you're choosing the person whose name local
parents will attach to Kaizen. The process is built to test **realness**
(do they execute or narrate?), **hat-swapping** (teach → recruit → sell →
retain → safety), and **trust** (minors on video; they'll hold the admin
console). The final judge is not you — it's the dashboard, during a paid
30-day trial.

## The process (4 steps, ~2 weeks + 30-day trial)

1. **20-min screen (video call)** — filter for the profile and the deal shape.
2. **75-min deep interview** — the question bank below, scored.
3. **References** — 2 parent references, 1 colleague. Fingerprinting where a
   payment rail requires it. (No third-party background check is run —
   review-queue item 6; never say one is.)
4. **Paid 30-day trial** — real sessions, dashboard-scored. The offer of the
   partnership comes only after this.

Decision rule at every step: **when unsure, advance only on evidence of
things they've *done*, never on quality of talk.**

---

## Step 1 — The screen (20 min)

Ask only four things:

1. "Walk me through the education operation you've run — what did *you*
   personally own?" (Listen for: schedules, parents, hiring, money — not
   'I was part of a team that…')
2. "This pays session wages plus profit share, not a salary, for at least a
   year. Founder takes nothing before you're paid. Does that work for your
   life right now?" (A hesitant yes is a no. A negotiation for base salary
   is a no *for this role* — kindly.)
3. "Are you available 4–8 PM on school nights?" (The business lives there.)
4. "We work with 13–17-year-olds on live video. Tell me how you've handled
   safeguarding responsibilities before." (You're listening for *specifics*
   — training taken, protocols followed, an incident handled — not vibes.)

Advance if: real operational ownership + clear-eyed yes on comp + window
availability + safeguarding fluency.

---

## Step 2 — The deep interview (75 min)

Score every answer 1–3 (1 = talk, 2 = plausible, 3 = evidence with names,
numbers, and consequences). Advance at 24+ of 33.

### A. Realness / execution (the core)

1. "Tell me about a program you started from zero. What existed on day 1,
   day 30, day 90?" — *3 looks like: specific first families, a number they
   tracked, a mistake they corrected mid-flight.*
2. "Describe a week where you were the teacher, the scheduler, and the
   salesperson at once. What actually happened?" — *tests hat-swapping as
   lived experience.*
3. "What's something you shut down or cancelled, and how did you decide?"
   — *our kill rule (any slot under 3 seats for 2 weeks dies) needs someone
   who can kill things without sentiment.*
4. "Sell me a standing seat, honestly." (Give them the real facts first: $550 a month, 2 × 75 min a week, 1:4, in person, one price for every payer; and separately Homework Hall: $14, up to 8
   students, one tutor rotating, NOT private tutoring.) — *the pass is
   honest framing: structured study time with a pro on call. The fail is
   overselling it as tutoring — that's our #1 refund generator, and if they
   oversell to YOU in an interview, they'll oversell to parents.*

### B. Parent trust & community

5. "Tell me about a skeptical parent you won over — and one you lost.
   What was the difference?" — *anyone who's never lost one is narrating.*
6. "You have zero marketing budget and 30 days to get 15 families to try a
   free session. Walk me through your actual week 1." — *3 = names of real
   channels they already hold (a PTA contact, a teacher group, a co-op),
   not 'social media.'*

### C. Retention instinct

7. "A family bought a membership and used 2 of 8 visits this month. What do
   you do, when, and what do you say?" — *3 = a personal call/note that
   concierges the habit ('want me to book Tuesdays?'), not a discount and
   not an email blast.*
8. "A kid no-shows twice. Walk me through it." — *3 = treats it as a churn
   signal and calls the parent warmly; 1 = policy enforcement talk.*

### D. Tutor supply & quality

9. "Who are the first three tutors you'd recruit, by name or by where
   you'd find them? What would each be great and weak at?" — *the profile
   we want arrives with a network. 3 = actual names/pools.*
10. "A tutor you personally recruited is getting lukewarm reviews. What
    happens next, concretely?" — *3 = observe a session, coach once,
    then cut kindly and fast; 1 = 'I'd give them more chances.'*

### E. Safety (disqualifying section — no partial credit)

11. "A student says something concerning about home life during a session.
    What do you do in the next hour? The next day?" — *must include:
    take it seriously, document, escalate per protocol, know mandatory-
    reporting basics for your state, never promise secrecy.* **Any
    casualness here ends the process regardless of total score.**

### The tell that overrides the scorecard

At the end, describe the 30-day trial and that the dashboard scores it.
Watch the reaction. The real ones **light up at being measured**. The
performers negotiate the criteria. That reaction is worth more than any
answer above.

## Step 3 — References (30 min total, scripts)

**Two parent references** (non-negotiable — parents, not managers):
- "Would you have trusted them alone, on video, with your kid for a year?"
- "Tell me about a time something went wrong. What did they do?"
- "If they started their own program, would you follow them to it?" —
  *this is the whole question. A pause is an answer.*

**One colleague/manager:**
- "When something was nobody's job, what did they do?"
- "What did they own that you never had to check on?"
- "Would you go into business with them with your own money?"

Plus: reference checks, and verify any center-director claims
with a quick call to the center.

---

## Step 4 — The paid 30-day trial (the real interview)

Pay them tutor wages for all of it. Agree the scoreboard in writing before
day 1 — it's the same dashboard the business runs on:

| Objective | Target | Dashboard number |
|---|---|---|
| Run the weekly free Community Hall | 4 sessions, ≥6 attendees avg | occupancy |
| Fill paid Homework Halls | 2 halls at ≥4 seats | seats/tutor-hour |
| Fill one Subject Clinic | ≥3 seats, tied to a real test date | clinic occupancy |
| Bring families back | ≥5 families attend a 2nd session | first→second conversion |
| Recruit | 2 tutor candidates into the vetting pipeline | admin roster |
| Quality | exit summaries written for every student, every session | ledger completeness |

**Green light** (offer the partnership): 5 of 6 hit, zero safety concerns,
and you'd happily leave them alone with the admin console.
**No** (pay them, thank them, part warmly): fewer than 4, or any
overselling of the Hall, or any safety casualness.
**The trap to avoid**: extending the trial because they're *likeable*.
The dashboard already told you.

## Red flags at any stage

- Wants to "redo the brand/strategy" before running a single session.
- Won't teach, or sees teaching as beneath the role.
- Salary-first negotiation after the deal shape was clear.
- Keeps three side commitments past month 3 with no wind-down plan.
- Talks scale before their first room is full.
- Any flexibility about the 13+ line or safety protocol "just this once."
