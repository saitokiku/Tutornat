# Club Director — compensation & benefits (the deal, in plain language)

> **Superseded (2026-09-02).** The deal that was actually struck is hourly with
> no profit share: [`PROGRAM_DIRECTOR.md`](./PROGRAM_DIRECTOR.md). Kept as the
> designed equity package, unsigned.

Design intent: pay real money for real hours from day one, share the actual
profits directly, and let them choose every quarter whether to take their
share as cash or convert it into ownership. Total transparency: the books
they're paid from are the same dashboard they run the business on.

> **Paper this with a lawyer before signing.** Profit share + hats like this
> is a partner-shaped relationship (likely an LLC membership or profit
> interest, not a W-2 role). The one-page terms below are the business deal;
> counsel turns it into the right instrument. Budget ~$1–2k once.

## 1. Cash from day one — session pay

- **$22–30/hr for every session they teach** (same flat-rate band as every
  Kaizen tutor; set the exact rate at hiring, review at 6 months).
- Paid monthly like any tutor, from the first trial day onward. This is
  guaranteed money for guaranteed work — it never depends on profits.

## 2. The profit share — paid directly from what we earn

**Base: 25% of operating profit, distributed quarterly.**

Operating profit is defined in writing, simply, and computed from the same
system that runs the business:

```
  Operating profit =
      all revenue actually collected (memberships + seats + 1:1)
    − tutor pay (including their own session pay)
    − platform costs (hosting, video, email, payment fees, AI)
    − insurance, accounting, legal
    − agreed shared marketing costs (e.g., the free Community Hall)
```

Rules that keep it honest:

- **Founder takes no salary or draw in year one until the profit share is
  paid.** Their 25% comes off the top of profit, not the leftovers.
- **Open books, always**: they see the admin business-metrics dashboard and
  the full P&L behind every distribution. No adjustments that weren't in the
  written definition.
- **Distributions happen when the business holds a safety buffer** of two
  months of fixed costs; profit above the buffer pays out quarterly. If a
  quarter is negative, nothing is owed by them — losses don't claw back
  (the founder carries downside; that's what the larger ownership is for).

**Milestone escalators** (written into the agreement, measured by the
dashboard):

| Milestone | Profit share becomes |
|---|---|
| Base | 25% |
| Green light: ≥12 committed seats held one full month by December | **30%** |
| Base case: ≥20 committed seats by March, churn < 5%/month | **35%** |

The escalators are permanent once earned. They pay for exactly the two
things that make this a business: seats held and seats kept. (Re-parameterised
2026-09-02 from memberships to the standing seat — `docs/STRATEGY.md` §5.1.)

## 3. Keep it or reinvest it — their choice, every quarter

Each distribution, they choose freely, in any split:

- **Take cash.** It's theirs. No strings, no vesting, no questions.
- **Reinvest** any portion to **buy real equity** in Kaizen Academy LLC at a
  price fixed in the agreement for the first two years (agree a modest,
  honest year-one valuation up front — e.g., $100–150k — so reinvestment is
  genuinely attractive), up to a **20% total ownership cap**.

Why this shape works: cash-takers are fairly paid for a great year; but a
believer converts profit into ownership at a founder-friendly price and
becomes, in economic reality, a co-founder. Their choice each quarter also
tells you exactly how much they believe — useful information you never have
to ask for.

## 4. Everything else they get

- **Founding-partner title** (Club Director, Founding Partner) and public
  credit — their name on the About page if they want it.
- **Full transparency**: admin console, live metrics, real P&L. They are
  never managing blind, and never wondering if the profit number is real.
- **Autonomy over their domain**: the five dashboard numbers, the tutor
  roster, the schedule, and the community channels are theirs to run.
  Founder keeps product/engineering, pricing, legal, and final say on
  safety. One page, signed, saying exactly that.
- **Flexible, remote, school-calendar shaped**: heavy Sep–May, light
  summers; hours concentrate 4–8 PM school nights by design.
- **Costs covered**: reference checks, fingerprinting where a payment rail
  requires it, liability insurance, any safeguarding training, and tools. They
  should never spend personal money to do this job. (Kaizen does not currently
  run third-party background checks — review-queue item 6; do not promise one.)
- **A real off-ramp** (fairness both ways): either side can end it with 30
  days' notice; they keep all cash distributions received, all vested/
  purchased equity, and session pay through the last day. No handcuffs —
  the deal is good enough that they stay because it's working.

## 5. Honest expectations (say this out loud when offering)

Year one at seat economics (`docs/STRATEGY.md` §5.1; re-run when the seat sim
exists): twelve seats at $550 in borrowed space is ~$6,600/mo revenue and
roughly $3,300/mo operating profit; at the 30% escalator that is ~$1,000/mo of
profit share on top of session pay. Twenty seats is ~$11,000/mo revenue and
~$6,000/mo profit; at 35% that is ~$2,100/mo. Below ~5 seats the room is
breakeven and the profit share pays little — their year-one money is mostly
session pay. We are offering a genuinely small first quarter and a genuinely
meaningful ownership path — the candidate this deal attracts is exactly the one
we want.
