# Wave 2 audit — every surface against the new geometry

**2026-09-02. Phase 1 of the Wave 2 rebuild. Diagnosis only; no code was changed
to produce it.** The companion documents are
`2026-09-02-wave1-product.md` (what was just built) and
`2026-09-02-wave2-geometry.md` (what this audit produced).

## The verdict in one paragraph

The product is well built and pointed at the wrong business. Its tokens, its
price discipline, its state machines and its comments are better than most
shipped software; there is not one typed dollar figure in the entire app tree
and the primitives that exist are documented with the reasoning behind them.
What it does not have is a **room**, a **cohort** or a **record** — the three
nouns the business now turns on. It has a study dashboard, a schedule of
drop-ins, and a marketing site whose loudest argument is still the free AI. The
seat exists as a price and a paragraph; it does not exist as an object any
surface can read, render, or act on. Nine of eleven surfaces are the wrong
shape for what Kaizen now sells.

## Method, and what it could not see

Eleven auditors each read one surface in full against the canon
(`CLAUDE.md`, `STRATEGY.md` §4.6/§5.1/§7, the Wave 1 spec, `CLAIMS_MATRIX.md`),
ranked findings P0/P1/P2, and named file and line. A twelfth pass read all
eleven reports at once with one instruction: kill what does not survive contact,
and find what a reader of one page could not see. Separately, every route was
rendered in a real browser at 1440x900 and 390x844 and inspected as images.

**It killed 11 findings**, which is the part worth trusting the rest for.
Examples: "gate the *certified teacher* claim behind a credential read" was
killed because the founder has already decided the other way — there is one
Program Director who *is* the credentialed tutor, so with n=1 the sentence is
true. "The first-month credit has no mechanism" was demoted because the admin
panel discloses to the operator that it is applied in Stripe by hand and the
claims matrix explicitly permits a runbook step. A "sign-in is a one-way trip"
P0 was killed outright: the return-path mechanism exists and is used in four
places.

**Three things it could not see, and they matter.** There is no `.env` in this
checkout, so every authenticated surface — `/dashboard`, `/admin`,
`/admin/funnel`, `/tutor` — rendered only its signed-out gate. Those four were
audited from source, not from pixels. Second, nothing was inspected with real
data in it, so density and overflow judgements on the console are inferred.
Third, the first screenshot pass captured blank pages below the fold and it was
a *capture artifact*, not a defect: `Reveal` deliberately fails toward visible
and its own comment documents that exact failure mode. The pass was redone under
`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`. Anything asserted here about rendering comes
from the second pass.

## Part 1 — What no single page could see

These eleven are the audit's real output. Each one spans surfaces, and each was
reported three or four times as a local copy problem by auditors who could not
see the shared cause.

### P0 · The exit rating is written into a dead end

`POST /api/tutoring/group/brief` is the only code path that turns a room into
evidence. It is finished, correct, and **has no caller anywhere in the product**
— `grep -rn "group/brief" web/app web/components` returns exactly one client
call, a GET for the prose brief. The Director's entire Wave 1 job is "write the
exit ratings in under two minutes" and there is no screen on which to do it.

Downstream, `group_observation` has exactly two readers in the whole web app:
the founder's funnel board and the next tutor's brief. Not `/family`, not
`/api/family/summary`, not `familySummary.js`, not `parentSummary.js`. So the
rating answers the parent's only question and never crosses the gap to her.
`/tutoring:461` sells that crossing in as many words.

**This is the single most important defect in the product.** The differentiator
is a loop with no input surface and no output surface.

### P0 · `familySummary` computes the record and `/family` throws it away

W4 shipped `headline` and `mastery` at the top level of `summarizeStudent`, with
a comment insisting "the demotion has to hold in the data, not only in the page
that happens to render it today." `app/family/page.js:681-731` renders `gpa`,
`streak` and `openAssignments` — the three numbers that comment demotes — and
never touches `mastery`. The one figure that makes $550 legible, "4 of 11
concepts confirmed, 2 moved this week", is computed, exported, spec-accepted and
dropped at the last inch. What the parent gets instead is the
`weekly_reports` narrative, which is an LLM summary of statistics **the browser
posted**, behind a modal.

### P0 · No surface prints a room's time in the room's timezone

`publicSchedule` returns `timezone` on every row and not one consumer reads it.
`/tutoring:215` and `Thread.js:19` format server-side, which on Vercel is UTC;
`ScheduleBrowser.js:45` formats in the visitor's browser zone. The same 6:00 PM
Austin room reads **"Tue 11:00 PM" server-rendered**, "4:00 PM" to a Californian
visitor, and "6:00 PM" to a parent already enrolled. Four surfaces, four
answers, for a business whose entire proposition is being in a particular room
on Thursday.

### P0 · `venue` is a column no public surface can reach

Added in migration 0033, set in the admin console, carried onto every
materialised room by `series.js:172`, rendered on `/family` — and **omitted from
`publicSchedule`'s select list**, which is the sole data path for `/`,
`/tutoring`, `/ai` and `/schedule`. Three auditors independently reported "this
page never says where the room is" as a copy failure on their own page. It is
one field name on one line, and it unblocks every storefront at once. Ranked P0
for cheapness, not severity: it answers the parent's literal first question.

Related and equally cheap: **the word "Austin" appears in zero user-facing
strings in the entire web app.** An in-person local business does not name its
city anywhere on its website.

### P0 · The seat's welcome email hands a $550 payer the AI dashboard

`billingEmails.js:16-33`: "Your Kaizen Standing Seat plan is active. Everything
the plan includes is available now — pick up where you left off," one button,
`/dashboard`. No day, no venue, no teacher, no "the Director will call you to
place your child." Between paying and being placed, a seat holder is a paying
customer with no room and no acknowledgement that one is coming — and the only
message they receive points at the product the strategy says is not the product.

### P1 · "Seat" names four different things, and the parent's page gets two backwards

`SEAT_PLAN` / `profiles.plan='seat'` is the $550 product. `group_session.kind =
'standing_seat'` is the cohort room. `standing_seats` is a recurring weekly
booking — used for **both** the $550 cohort and a member's free Homework Hall.
`group_seat` is one student in one room. The most important noun in the business
means its opposite on the payer's own screen, and the table reuse is why. It
will also break the founder's board the first time anyone counts
`standing_seats` without filtering by series kind.

### P1 · The seat is 2 sessions a week; a cohort is one row with one weekday

`SEAT_PLAN.seat.sessionsPerWeek = 2`, and `series.js` states the constraint
plainly: "one weekday, one local wall-clock time." So a seat requires the
Director to create two series and enrol each family twice, and `/family` renders
the result as two unrelated weekly places, each with its own **unconfirmed,
one-way "End weekly place" button**. There is no object in the system that is
"the Tuesday-and-Thursday Algebra cohort."

### P1 · No seat-cohort read path exists anywhere in `lib/server/`

`publicSchedule` hard-excludes `.neq('kind','standing_seat')` — correctly, since
seat rooms are reserved inventory. That exclusion was then silently taken as a
rule about *visibility* rather than about *purchasability*. Until one
seat-shaped read exists (pattern, venue, lead tutor, places remaining, no
purchase path) every seat section on every page can only be prose, and the three
pages will keep drifting apart.

### P1 · `club_interest.kind` is a vocabulary nobody agreed on

Four surfaces pass four incompatible things: `null` (landing, `/tutoring`),
`'pricing'`, `'ai_plans'`, `'ai_solo'`/`'ai_hall'`. Two are page names, two are
SKU names. The funnel board's first row therefore cannot separate $550 intent
from $14 intent — the one segmentation the whole Wave 1 plan turns on. Three
auditors each proposed a different fix for their own page; the actual defect is
that no one owns the enum.

### P1 · The same not-configured failure is designed four incompatible ways

`/diagnostic` discriminates four causes with four operator-voice strings;
`/pricing` collapses "Stripe unset" and "club closed" into one CTA; `/tutoring`
silently swaps a CTA and says nothing; the landing page's bare `catch {}` turns
a database outage into a page **pixel-identical to a healthy pre-launch
storefront**, with nothing logged. Hard Rule 6 is satisfied four times and
consistent zero times, and the founder cannot tell an outage from a quiet week.

### P1 · The thing no surface provides: an absence and make-up policy

The seat is sold as "yours whether or not they turn up", metered by calendar
month with no rollover, and advertised as "2 a week" and "9 a month" in the same
card. What happens when a child misses two weeks is the first question after
"how much" for a reserved monthly commitment, and it is answered nowhere — not
on `/tutoring`, not on `/pricing`, not in `/terms`. The seat page constructs the
objection and walks away from it.

## Part 2 — Surface by surface

| Surface | For | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `/` | parent | **wrong shape** — the AI is still the argument by volume |
| `/tutoring` | parent | needs work — the best seat argument in the product, on top of a timezone bug |
| `/pricing`, `/ai` | visitor | **wrong shape** — features an unbuyable tier, advertises cut voice |
| `/diagnostic` | parent | needs work — right ceremony, no child, no report, no capture |
| `/schedule`, `/tutors` | parent | **wrong shape** — a marketplace for a business with one tutor |
| `/dashboard` | student | **wrong shape** — five tabs, none of them the seat |
| `/family` | parent | **wrong shape** — sells a retired membership to the person paying $550 |
| `/admin` | director | **wrong shape** — sixteen panels, none of them tonight |
| `/admin/funnel` | founder | needs work — seven counters, no conversion |
| `/tutor` | tutor | **wrong shape** — the room-running loop is the fourth panel |

### The four that matter most

**`/family` is the worst, because of who it belongs to.** Its subhead sells
"your membership's included visits"; its month is summarised as Homework Hall
visits and an **included private session** — one retired product and one cut
one; it offers "Book 1:1" and "your first session is on us" (both cut); it can
never render its allowance card for a seat family because `CLUB_VIEW` omits
`club_seat_included`; and `PLAN_NAME` has no `seat` key, so a $550 holder's own
page cannot name what they bought. It answers none of the parent's three
questions.

**`/admin` has no view of tonight.** Sixteen panels, and `ClassesSection` — the
only room surface — filters `.gt('scheduled_start', now)`, so a room in progress
is invisible. The console models the *catalogue* of rooms and never the
*occasion*. The four stat cards at the top are Users, AI cost, Open handoffs,
Open support: the AI-first product's stat row surviving verbatim. `seat` is not
in the Grant plan picker and the route refuses it. The kill-switch `toggle()`
flips local state and **ignores the response entirely** — a 501, a 400 or an
expired session all leave the switch showing the wrong position.

**`/dashboard` has no concept of a seat.** Five tabs, and not one reads
`profiles.plan`, `standing_seats`, or the next in-person session — data that is
already served by an endpoint it already calls. It fetches `/api/engine/state`
and keeps only `summary`, discarding `growthTip`, `next` and `focus` including a
focus **a parent set**. Its Growth tab advertises private 1:1 video with a
primary button. The word "Mastery" on the student's screen is attached to an
assisted, model-graded, client-held SM-2 number that never touches the evidence
ledger.

**`/tutor` buries the only loop that matters.** The room-running panel is
fourth, under two payout stat cards and a public-profile editor. Every
`standing_seat` room is badged **"Clinic"** because `KIND_BADGE` has no entry for
it. `markAttendance` ignores its response. The exit-summary form holds four
fields in local state with no draft persistence and no dirty guard, only one can
be open at a time, and opening a second destroys the first — for the two minutes
of work the entire differentiator depends on. Its "Recommended next step" picker
offers `private` — a cut product.

## Part 3 — What is genuinely good and must survive

A redesign that loses these would be a downgrade.

- **The token layer.** One type scale with tracking and leading travelling with
  the size, and **zero arbitrary `text-[Npx]` in the entire app tree**. The
  palette is declared as channel triplets so every opacity utility works and
  dark mode is a variable flip; zero `rgba()`, zero default-Tailwind colours.
- **Price discipline.** Not one typed dollar figure anywhere in `app/` or
  `components/`, enforced by `priceTruth.test.mjs`. It held under three
  repricings.
- **The comments.** `Reveal.js` explains why server HTML must never carry
  `opacity:0` and encodes it; `Button.js` explains why disabled drops to
  `panel2/muted`. This is the repo's best asset and the reason the audit could
  tell deliberate decisions from accidents.
- **`InterestForm`** — the best-built state machine in the codebase:
  idle/busy/done/error, inline failure, honeypot, `aria-describedby`.
- **The unpaid-room nag** in `TutorClasses.js:161`. It names the consequence in
  the Director's own terms, computes a real deadline from the sweep's own rule,
  is itself the button that fixes it, and clears where it appeared. **This is
  the exact template the exit ratings need and do not have.**
- **The supervision/tutoring firewall** on `/tutoring`, stated four times in four
  registers. Review-queue item 20 is honoured better there than anywhere.
- **`/tutoring`'s "Start from your problem"** — six parent sentences mapped to
  six products with six prices. The best information design in the product.
- **The server-side help-queue sort** in `roster/route.js`: red → yellow → green,
  longest-waiting first, computed where the client cannot get it wrong.
- **`Thread`'s empty state**, which refuses to dress placeholders as a booked
  week, and `/diagnostic`'s "What it does not tell you", which volunteers the
  product's own limits.

## Part 4 — What the design system lacks

`components/ui/` has eleven primitives and no noun for the business. It needs:
a **RoomCard** (kind badge, day and time in the room's own zone, duration, ratio,
named lead tutor, venue as a first-class line, seats held over capacity) —
currently hand-drawn five different ways; a **VenueLine**; a **KindBadge**, since
the kind→label→colour map is retyped in **eight places with three different
labels for the same room**; a **MasteryLine** for "N of M confirmed"; an
**AttendanceControl**; a **Dialog**, because eleven hand-rolled overlays each
independently omitted `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal`, focus trap and focus
restore; and **EmptyState** and **Skeleton**, both currently defined twice,
locally, differently.

Two palette leaks: every transactional email and both standalone HTML pages
render in the **retired** palette, and `app/icon.svg` is a **third** palette
including `sakura`, a colour the one-system spec explicitly retired.

## What this audit does not recommend

It does not recommend rebuilding the design language, changing the framework,
touching the engine's scheduler or estimator, "fixing" the last-write-wins sync,
un-retiring the memberships, reviving 1:1, or making the AI louder. The system
is sound. What it describes is a product whose furniture is arranged for a
business that no longer exists.
