# Anti-slop — the standard and the plan (2026-08-21)

The brief: make every word of ad copy read like a person thought hard before
writing it, and make the whole product — frontend and backend — free of the
patterns that read as machine-generated filler. This document is the thought
process; the diffs that follow it are the execution.

## What "slop" actually is in THIS codebase

We measured before writing, because the answer is not what the word usually
means. The classic tells are absent: zero hits for unlock / empower / seamless
/ effortless / elevate / journey across every marketing surface, em-dash
density around two per page, no exclamation-mark enthusiasm, no fake urgency.
Whoever wrote the Day/Night copy was already allergic to that register.

The slop here is one layer down, and it is three specific things:

**1. One rhetorical move, cloned.** The contrastive correction — "X, not Y",
"never does Z" — appears fourteen-plus times across four marketing pages.
"A tutor, *not* an answer machine." "It teaches, never cheats." "Never does
the work for them" — that exact clause three times on three surfaces. The move
is good; that is why the model reached for it. But a writer who has actually
thought about each claim makes each claim in the shape that claim needs:
sometimes a contrast, sometimes a mechanism, sometimes a number, sometimes a
concession. Fourteen contrasts in a row is a template wearing a voice.

**2. A metaphor that hardened into a house style.** "Gap" appears nineteen
times on the landing page and titles two heroes. A metaphor earns its keep the
first time and starts costing on the fifth: the reader stops seeing tutoring
between classes and starts seeing a branding exercise.

**3. Claims that pose instead of argue.** "It teaches, never cheats" is a
posture. The Exchange transcript four sections lower — a real Socratic
exchange where the model declines to hand over the answer — is an argument.
The mature version of this site leads with the mechanism and lets the reader
draw the conclusion, instead of asserting the conclusion and hoping. This is
also the register parents (the actual payer, a skeptical audience being
burned right now by AI-cheating headlines) extend trust to: show me, don't
tell me.

And one claim is simply not honest yet: the marquee promises ten subjects
(Algebra … SAT Prep … Spanish) while verified mastery exists for eight
middle-school math concepts and the Algebra I bank sits in review, and the
human tutoring that could justify the breadth reading has zero hired tutors.
CLAIMS_MATRIX open item 4 already forbids exactly this. It ships anyway.

## The standard (what replaces it)

The repo already contains the target voice — in the Terms, of all places:
*"Homework Hall is shared academic support, not private tutoring performed
for several people at once: students work independently and the tutor rotates
through the room clearing blockers."* Specific, mechanical, unafraid to state
the limit, and therefore believable. The marketing should read like the Terms
with warmth. Concretely:

- **Every claim gets the shape it needs.** Contrast is rationed: at most one
  "X, not Y" per page, spent where the contrast IS the point. Everything else
  argues by mechanism ("the tutor asks what you'd do first"), by number
  ($14, 8 students, Tuesday 5 PM), or by concession ("we don't promise
  grades; nobody honest can").
- **Metaphors are single-use.** "Gap" survives in at most the one place it
  works hardest. Everywhere else says the literal thing: between class and
  the test, at 9 PM with homework due.
- **Show before tell.** Where a claim has a demonstrable mechanism (the
  Socratic refusal, the mastery check delay, the roster), the demonstration
  leads and the assertion shrinks or goes.
- **No claim the code doesn't enforce.** The same rule the backend learned
  the hard way — six verification rounds caught comments promising paths that
  didn't exist — applies to copy verbatim. If the claims matrix can't source
  it, it doesn't ship. The marquee becomes true: help with whatever you
  bring; *verified* mastery where the bank actually reaches, named plainly.
- **Sentence music varies.** Read each page aloud; where three sentences in a
  row share a skeleton, one changes or dies.

## The plan

**Wave A — copy (this branch, now).** Rewrite the marketing surfaces against
the standard: `/` (both heroes, the marquee and its honest replacement, the
proof strip captions), `/ai`, `/tutoring`, `/about`, `/pricing`, `/tutors`,
`/tutors/apply`, `/schedule`, `/contact`, the dn components that carry prose
(Exchange framing, Thread captions, InterestForm, AiLadder), and the
transactional emails' marketing-adjacent lines. Constraints: claims tests
stay green, prices stay interpolated, CLAIMS_MATRIX rows updated where a
claim changes, the night/day worlds keep their registers. Each rewrite states
in its commit what argument replaced what pose.

**Wave B — frontend structure (same branch).** The audit's deferred
consolidation, which is the visual half of the same disease: the fourth
ad-hoc system re-deriving primitives (~40 hand-rolled inputs → `.k-input`,
done; the remainder swept here), `k-btn-ink` renamed to what it is, the nine
eyebrow-label variants reduced to the two sanctioned recipes, the legacy
cream palette in transactional emails replaced with the live tokens, text
glyphs (✕ ✓) replaced by the icon system where Icons.js already covers them,
and the free session called ONE name everywhere (it currently has six).

**Wave C — backend prose and truth (same branch).** Slop in a backend is a
comment that narrates instead of explains, a claim the code doesn't keep, a
payload field nothing reads, an error message that apologizes instead of
directing. Sweep: comments asserting behavior verified false (the recurring
bug class of this whole audit), dead payload fields (`resumed`,
`calibrationGap`, `staffCount` where unrendered — render or remove), error
copy voice (state what happened and the next step, never "oops"), and naming
drift in code mirroring the six-names problem.

**Wave D — verify.** The proven loop: adversarial re-read of the rewritten
copy against the standard AND the claims matrix; a browser pass confirming no
layout broke under different copy lengths; the full suite.

**Deliberately out of scope:** promoting the Algebra I items (owner's review,
one message away — `docs/reviews/ALGEBRA1_ITEM_BANK.md`), and any new visual
identity. This plan removes template residue from the identity the site
already has; it does not invent a fifth system.
