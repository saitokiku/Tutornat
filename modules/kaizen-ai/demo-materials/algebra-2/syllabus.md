## Course Description

Algebra II builds on the foundations of Algebra I and Geometry to deepen students'
understanding of functions and to prepare them for Precalculus and college-level
mathematics. Over the semester students study families of functions — linear,
quadratic, polynomial, rational, radical, exponential, and logarithmic — and learn to
model real situations with them. Students strengthen their algebraic fluency
(factoring, completing the square, working with rational expressions and radicals),
develop symbolic and graphical reasoning, and finish the term with an introduction to
sequences, series, probability, and statistics.

The course emphasizes three habits: (1) representing a problem in multiple forms
(equation, table, graph, words); (2) justifying each algebraic step; and (3) checking
whether an answer is reasonable. Students who complete Algebra II should be able to
analyze a function's domain, range, and behavior, solve equations exactly and
approximately, and communicate mathematical reasoning clearly.

## Units

The course is organized into **8 units**:

1. **Functions & Their Graphs** — function notation, domain and range, evaluating
   functions, transformations of parent graphs.
2. **Quadratic Functions & Equations** — factoring, completing the square, the
   quadratic formula, the discriminant, complex roots.
3. **Polynomial Functions** — end behavior, zeros and multiplicity, dividing
   polynomials, the Factor and Remainder Theorems.
4. **Rational Expressions & Equations** — simplifying, multiplying, adding, and
   solving rational equations; asymptotes.
5. **Radicals & Rational Exponents** — nth roots, rational exponents, simplifying
   radicals, solving radical equations.
6. **Exponential & Logarithmic Functions** — exponential growth and decay,
   logarithms, laws of logarithms, solving exponential and logarithmic equations.
7. **Sequences & Series** — arithmetic and geometric sequences, summation notation,
   arithmetic and geometric series.
8. **Probability & Statistics** — counting principles, probability, measures of
   center and spread, the normal distribution.

## Course Policies

**Late work.** Homework may be turned in up to **two class days late for 70% credit**.
After that, late homework is not accepted except in the case of an excused absence. If
you are absent, you have as many days as you were out to submit missed work at full
credit. Quizzes and exams missed for an excused absence must be made up within one
week, by appointment.

**Academic integrity.** All quiz and exam work must be your own. On homework you may
discuss ideas with classmates, but the work you submit must be written in your own
words and reflect your own understanding. Copying another student's work, using an
answer key or online solver to produce submitted work, or sharing answers during an
assessment is a violation of the Lincoln High School honor code and will result in a
zero on the assignment and a referral. Show your steps: an answer with no supporting
work may not receive full credit.

**Calculator policy.** A scientific calculator is required; a graphing calculator
(TI-84 or equivalent) is recommended and will be used in class. Calculators are
**allowed on homework and on the calculator-permitted sections of quizzes and exams as
marked**. The Midterm and Final each have a no-calculator section (basic skills:
factoring, simplifying, evaluating logs of nice values) and a calculator-permitted
section. **Phones may not be used as calculators** during any assessment.

**Extra help.** Come to office hours (Tue/Thu 3:15–4:00) or email to set up a time.
Asking for help early is a sign of a strong student, not a weak one.
