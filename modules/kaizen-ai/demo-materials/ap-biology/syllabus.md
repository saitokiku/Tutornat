## Course Description

AP Biology is a rigorous, college-level course that explores the core principles of
modern biology. Following the College Board AP Biology Course and Exam Description,
the course is organized around eight units and four "big ideas": **Evolution**,
**Energetics**, **Information Storage and Transmission**, and **Systems Interactions**.

Students move from the molecular scale (the chemistry of water, biomolecules, and
enzymes) up through cells, energy transformation, genetics, molecular biology,
evolution, and ecology. Throughout the term we emphasize the six AP Science Practices:
concept explanation, visual representations, questions and methods, data
representation and analysis, statistical tests and data interpretation, and argumentation.
Roughly 25% of class time is devoted to hands-on inquiry laboratory work.

By the end of the course, students will be able to reason quantitatively about
biological systems, design and interpret experiments, and construct evidence-based
arguments. This course prepares students for the AP Biology Exam and for
introductory college biology.

**Prerequisites:** Completion of Biology and Chemistry (or concurrent enrollment in
Chemistry) with a grade of B or higher.

## Tracked Course Concepts (Units)

The course tracks the following eight core concepts. Assignments, labs, and exams are
tagged to these concepts.

1. **Chemistry of Life / Biomolecules** — water, carbon, carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, nucleic acids, enzymes
2. **Cell Structure & Function** — organelles, membranes, transport, compartmentalization
3. **Cellular Energetics** — Photosynthesis and Cellular Respiration; ATP and energy transfer
4. **Cell Communication & the Cell Cycle** — signal transduction, feedback, mitosis, regulation
5. **Heredity & Mendelian Genetics** — segregation, independent assortment, Punnett squares, non-Mendelian inheritance
6. **Gene Expression & Regulation** — DNA replication, transcription, translation, operons, eukaryotic regulation
7. **Natural Selection & Evolution** — mechanisms of evolution, Hardy-Weinberg, evidence, speciation
8. **Ecology** — population, community, and ecosystem dynamics; energy flow and biogeochemical cycles

## Course Policies

### Lab Safety
Safety is non-negotiable. Students must:
- Wear approved goggles and aprons whenever chemicals, heat, or biological specimens are in use.
- Never eat, drink, or apply cosmetics in the lab.
- Tie back long hair and avoid loose clothing near open flames.
- Report all spills, breakage, and injuries to Mr. Chen immediately.
- Follow all disposal instructions; never pour reagents down the drain unless directed.
- Read each lab handout **before** class and complete the pre-lab questions.

A signed lab safety contract is required before any student may participate in
laboratory work. **Students without a signed contract will complete an alternative
written assignment and forfeit lab points.**

### Late Work
- Homework and lab reports are due at the start of class on the due date.
- Late work loses **10% per school day**, up to a maximum of 3 days (30%). After
  3 days, late work is not accepted and receives a 0.
- Missing work due to an **excused absence** may be submitted within the number of
  days absent without penalty. It is the student's responsibility to check the class
  calendar and arrange make-up work.
- Tests and exams missed for excused absences must be made up within one week, by
  appointment during office hours.

### Academic Integrity
All submitted work must be your own. Collaboration on homework is encouraged for
*studying and discussion*, but every student must write their own answers and lab
analyses in their own words. The following are violations and result in a **0 on the
assignment**, a parent/guardian conference, and a referral per the Lincoln High School
Honor Code:
- Copying another student's work or allowing your work to be copied.
- Plagiarism from any source, including AI text generators, without attribution.
- Bringing unauthorized materials into a test, or sharing test content across periods.
- Fabricating or falsifying laboratory data.

Using an AI study tutor (such as Kaizen) to **explain concepts, quiz yourself, and
check your reasoning** is encouraged. Submitting AI-generated text as your own written
work is not.

### Attendance & Participation
Regular attendance is essential in a fast-paced AP course. Students are expected to
participate actively in discussions and labs and to come prepared each day.

### Extra Help & AP Exam
Office hours are Tuesdays and Thursdays 3:15–4:15 PM. The AP Biology Exam is
administered in May; this fall course is the first semester of a full-year sequence
that concludes with AP Exam preparation in the spring.
