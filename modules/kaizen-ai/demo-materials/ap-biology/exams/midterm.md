# Answer Key — Midterm Exam (Units 1–4)

**Section I:** 1) **B**  2) **C**  3) **C**  4) **B**  5) **C**  6) **A**

**Section II**
7. The enzyme binds substrate at its active site and stabilizes the transition state, **lowering the activation energy** so the reaction proceeds faster; it is not consumed. Denaturing factors: **extreme temperature** and **extreme pH** (also high salt) — they disrupt the bonds holding the active site's shape.
8. **Passive transport** moves substances down their concentration gradient with no ATP (e.g., diffusion/osmosis, facilitated diffusion). **Active transport** moves substances against the gradient and **requires ATP** (e.g., the sodium–potassium pump).
9. Interphase (G1, S — DNA replication, G2) grows the cell and copies DNA. Mitosis: **prophase** (chromosomes condense), **metaphase** (chromosomes line up at the middle), **anaphase** (sister chromatids separate to opposite poles), **telophase** (nuclei re-form), then cytokinesis. A **checkpoint** (e.g., G2/M) verifies conditions (DNA replicated/undamaged) before allowing the cell to proceed, preventing errors.

**Section III**
10. (a) ATP production by oxidative phosphorylation **stops**, because electrons can no longer flow to O₂ and the chain backs up. (b) The proton gradient **collapses/dissipates** — no more H⁺ are pumped, so ATP synthase loses its driving force. (c) Fermentation regenerates **NAD⁺** so glycolysis can keep making a small amount of ATP (2 net per glucose) without oxygen; it produces far less ATP because glucose is only partially broken down and the large ATP yield of the Krebs cycle and ETC is lost.
