## Section I — Multiple Choice (5 pts each)

4. Which two molecules produced in the light reactions power the Calvin cycle?
 A) ATP and NADPH  B) glucose and O₂  C) CO₂ and H₂O  D) NADH and FADH₂

## Section II — Short Answer (15 pts each)

6. Trace the path of a glucose molecule's energy through aerobic respiration. Name the three stages in order, where each occurs, and the main products of each.

## Section III — Free Response (25 pts)

8. A researcher measures the rate of photosynthesis (O₂ released) by an aquatic plant under varying light intensity and two CO₂ levels (low and high).
 (a) Predict how increasing light intensity affects the rate, and explain why.
 (b) Predict which CO₂ level gives a higher maximum rate, and explain in terms of the Calvin cycle.
 (c) Describe one factor other than light and CO₂ that could limit the rate, and explain its effect.

# Answer Key — Unit 3 Test

**Section I:** 1) **B**  2) **C**  3) **B**  4) **A**  5) **B**

**Section II**
6. **Glycolysis** (cytoplasm): glucose → 2 pyruvate, net 2 ATP + 2 NADH. **Krebs cycle** (mitochondrial matrix): pyruvate fully oxidized → CO₂, ATP, NADH, FADH₂. **Oxidative phosphorylation / ETC** (inner mitochondrial membrane): NADH/FADH₂ donate electrons, O₂ is the final acceptor, ~26–34 ATP made by chemiosmosis. Total ≈ 30–38 ATP.
7. Photosynthesis uses CO₂ + H₂O + light to make glucose + O₂; respiration uses glucose + O₂ to make CO₂ + H₂O + ATP. The outputs of each are the inputs of the other, cycling carbon and oxygen and transferring energy from sunlight to ATP.

**Section III**
8. (a) Higher light intensity → faster rate (more energy for the light reactions → more ATP/NADPH), until it plateaus when another factor limits. (b) **High CO₂** gives a higher maximum rate because the Calvin cycle needs CO₂ as its carbon source; more substrate lets carbon fixation proceed faster once light is sufficient. (c) **Temperature** (affects enzyme activity such as Rubisco) — too low slows enzymes, too high denatures them; either lowers the rate. (Water availability or chlorophyll amount also acceptable.)
