# Answer Key — HW2 Cellular Energetics

**Part A**
1. C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + energy (ATP). (Glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + ATP.)
2. **Glycolysis** — cytoplasm/cytosol; **Krebs (citric acid) cycle** — mitochondrial matrix; **oxidative phosphorylation / electron transport chain** — inner mitochondrial membrane.
3. Glycolysis ≈ 2 ATP net; Krebs ≈ 2 ATP; oxidative phosphorylation ≈ 26–34 ATP; **total ≈ 30–38 ATP** (commonly stated ~36).
4. Oxygen accepts the electrons at the end of the ETC and combines with H⁺ to form water, keeping the chain "pulling" electrons. Without O₂ the chain backs up, the proton gradient collapses, and ATP production by oxidative phosphorylation stops.
5. Fermentation regenerates **NAD⁺** from NADH so that glycolysis can keep running (glycolysis needs NAD⁺). It yields only the 2 ATP from glycolysis, but that lets the cell make some ATP without oxygen.

**Part B**
6. 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂.
7. **Light reactions** — thylakoid membranes (require light directly); **Calvin cycle (light-independent)** — stroma.
8. **ATP and NADPH.**
9. NADPH provides electrons/reducing power and ATP provides energy to **fix and reduce CO₂** into sugar; the output is **G3P**, which forms **glucose**.

**Part C**
10. Photosynthesis's products (glucose + O₂) are respiration's reactants; respiration's products (CO₂ + H₂O) are photosynthesis's reactants. Energy captured from sunlight and stored in glucose is later released by respiration — the two cycle matter (carbon, oxygen) and transfer energy through the ecosystem.
11. (a) As light intensity increases, the photosynthetic rate **increases** (disks rise faster / less time). (b) At high light another factor limits the rate — e.g., **CO₂ concentration**, temperature, or the amount of chlorophyll/enzymes (Rubisco). Any one is acceptable.
