# Answer Key — HW1 Biomolecules

**Part A**
1. Water is polar (partial negative O, partial positive H), so molecules form **hydrogen bonds** with one another. Cohesion (water-to-water) produces surface tension and lets a column of water be pulled up a plant's xylem; adhesion (water-to-other-surfaces) helps water cling to vessel walls, aiding capillary action.
2. Carbohydrates → **monosaccharides**; lipids → (not true polymers, but built from) **fatty acids + glycerol**; proteins → **amino acids**; nucleic acids → **nucleotides**.
3. Dehydration synthesis **builds** polymers by removing a molecule of **water** to form a bond; hydrolysis **breaks** polymers by adding water. Dehydration builds.
4. Saturated fatty acids have no C=C double bonds (straight chains that pack tightly → solid, e.g., butter); unsaturated have one or more double bonds that create kinks preventing tight packing → liquid (oils).
5. Primary structure = the **sequence of amino acids**; tertiary = the overall **3-D folded shape** of a single polypeptide. Stabilized by hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, hydrophobic interactions, and disulfide bridges (any one).

**Part B**
6. Extreme pH disrupts the ionic and hydrogen bonds holding the protein's shape, causing **denaturation** — the active site loses its specific shape and can no longer bind substrate, so the enzyme stops working.
7. (a) The enzyme **lowers the activation energy**, letting the reaction proceed faster at a given temperature. (b) The enzyme binds substrate at its active site and is **released unchanged** after products form, so it can catalyze the reaction repeatedly.
8. At high substrate concentration nearly all enzyme active sites are occupied — the enzyme is **saturated**. Adding more substrate cannot increase the rate until existing enzyme–substrate complexes turn over, so the rate plateaus (limited by enzyme amount).

**Part C**
9. (a) Rate **increases** with temperature as molecules move faster and collide more often. (b) After 37 °C the rate **drops sharply** because the enzyme **denatures** — heat breaks the bonds maintaining the active site's shape. (c) Optimal ≈ **37 °C**, which matches normal human body temperature.
