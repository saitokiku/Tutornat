# Answer Key — Homework 4

**Part A**
1. DNA is transcribed into RNA, which is translated into protein (DNA → RNA → protein).
2. Transcription occurs in the **nucleus**; translation occurs in the **cytoplasm** (at ribosomes, free or on the rough ER).
3. RNA polymerase reads the DNA template strand and synthesizes a complementary mRNA strand (5'→3').
4. **Exons** are the coding segments retained in mature mRNA; **introns** are non-coding segments spliced out during RNA processing.

**Part B**
5. AUG = Met (start), GCU = Ala, UUU = Phe, UAA = stop → **Met–Ala–Phe** (then translation stops).
6. Redundant/degenerate means most amino acids are specified by more than one codon. A point mutation in the third ("wobble") position often gives a codon for the **same** amino acid (a silent mutation), so the protein is unchanged.

**Part C**
7. **Initiation** (ribosome assembles at the start codon), **elongation** (tRNAs add amino acids as the ribosome moves codon by codon), **termination** (a stop codon releases the finished polypeptide).
8. **tRNA** carries amino acids; its **anticodon** base-pairs with the complementary mRNA codon.

**Part D**
9. **Lactose present:** it (via allolactose) binds the repressor, which releases the operator, so RNA polymerase transcribes the lac genes and the cell can digest lactose. **Lactose absent:** the repressor binds the operator and blocks transcription. This lets the cell make the enzymes only when the substrate is there — it doesn't waste energy.
10. Any two: chromatin/histone modification, DNA methylation, RNA splicing (alternative splicing), mRNA stability/degradation control, or regulation of translation and post-translational modification.

**Part E**
11. A single-nucleotide **deletion** is a frameshift: every codon downstream is read in a shifted **reading frame**, so essentially all amino acids after the deletion are wrong and a premature stop codon usually appears — typically a nonfunctional protein. A single-nucleotide **substitution** in the third codon position often changes nothing (silent, due to code redundancy) or at most one amino acid (missense), so its effect is usually far smaller.
