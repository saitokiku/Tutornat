# Chapter 3 — Heredity & Mendelian Genetics

**Concept:** Mendelian Genetics · **Unit 5**

## Mendel's Foundations

In the 1860s, Gregor Mendel bred pea plants and discovered the basic rules of
inheritance. His work introduced key terms still used today:

- **Gene** — a unit of heredity that codes for a trait.
- **Allele** — one version of a gene (e.g., an allele for purple flowers vs. white).
- **Genotype** — an organism's genetic makeup (e.g., *Pp*).
- **Phenotype** — the observable trait (e.g., purple flowers).
- **Homozygous** — two identical alleles (*PP* or *pp*).
- **Heterozygous** — two different alleles (*Pp*).
- **Dominant** — an allele whose phenotype masks the other in a heterozygote (written
  uppercase, *P*).
- **Recessive** — an allele expressed only when homozygous (lowercase, *p*).

## Mendel's Two Laws

**The Law of Segregation:** Each organism carries two alleles for each gene, and the two
alleles **separate** during gamete formation so that each gamete receives only one. This
happens physically during **meiosis I**, when homologous chromosomes separate.

**The Law of Independent Assortment:** Alleles of different genes (on different
chromosome pairs) are distributed to gametes **independently** of one another. This is why
a dihybrid cross produces new combinations of traits.

## Punnett Squares and the Monohybrid Cross

A **Punnett square** predicts offspring genotypes by combining possible gametes. Consider
a cross between two heterozygous purple-flowered plants (*Pp × Pp*):

|      | **P** | **p** |
|------|-------|-------|
| **P** | PP    | Pp    |
| **p** | Pp    | pp    |

- **Genotypic ratio:** 1 *PP* : 2 *Pp* : 1 *pp*
- **Phenotypic ratio:** 3 purple : 1 white (because *P* is dominant)

This classic **3:1** phenotypic ratio is the signature of a monohybrid cross between two
heterozygotes. A **test cross** (crossing an unknown dominant phenotype with a homozygous
recessive) reveals the unknown genotype: all dominant offspring suggest homozygous
dominant, while a 1:1 ratio reveals heterozygous.

## The Dihybrid Cross

When two genes are followed at once, use the Law of Independent Assortment. Cross two
plants heterozygous for seed shape (round *R* dominant to wrinkled *r*) and seed color
(yellow *Y* dominant to green *y*): **RrYy × RrYy**.

Each parent makes four gamete types (*RY, Ry, rY, ry*). A 4×4 Punnett square (16 boxes)
gives the famous dihybrid **phenotypic ratio of 9:3:3:1**:

- **9** round yellow (R_Y_)
- **3** round green (R_yy)
- **3** wrinkled yellow (rrY_)
- **1** wrinkled green (rryy)

A useful shortcut is the **rule of multiplication**: because the genes assort
independently, treat each gene separately (each is a 3:1 cross) and multiply the
probabilities. For example, the chance of round *and* yellow = 3/4 × 3/4 = 9/16.

### Labeled Example: Probability Without Drawing 16 Boxes
*Question:* In the cross *RrYy × RrYy*, what fraction of offspring are wrinkled and
yellow (rr Y_)?
1. Probability of wrinkled (rr) from *Rr × Rr* = **1/4**.
2. Probability of yellow (Y_) from *Yy × Yy* = **3/4**.
3. Multiply: 1/4 × 3/4 = **3/16**. ✓ (matches the "3" in 9:3:3:1)

## Beyond Simple Dominance: Non-Mendelian Inheritance

Real inheritance is often more complex than Mendel's dominant/recessive model.

- **Incomplete dominance** — the heterozygote shows an **intermediate** phenotype.
  Crossing red (*C^R C^R*) and white (*C^W C^W*) snapdragons yields **pink** (*C^R C^W*)
  offspring. A pink × pink cross gives a **1 red : 2 pink : 1 white** phenotypic ratio
  (phenotype now matches genotype).
- **Codominance** — **both** alleles are fully and separately expressed in the
  heterozygote. In the human **ABO blood group**, alleles *I^A* and *I^B* are codominant,
  so genotype *I^A I^B* produces type **AB** blood (both A and B antigens). Allele *i* is
  recessive; type O is *ii*.
- **Multiple alleles** — more than two allele versions exist in a population (the ABO
  system has three: *I^A*, *I^B*, *i*), though any individual carries only two.
- **Polygenic inheritance** — many genes contribute to one trait (e.g., human skin color,
  height), producing continuous variation rather than distinct categories.
- **Pleiotropy** — a single gene affects multiple traits (e.g., sickle-cell allele).
- **Sex-linked inheritance** — genes on the X chromosome show different inheritance
  patterns in males and females. Because males are **XY** (hemizygous), a single recessive
  X-linked allele (e.g., for red-green colorblindness or hemophilia) is expressed; these
  conditions are more common in males.
- **Epistasis** — one gene masks or modifies the expression of another gene (e.g., a gene
  for coat pigment deposition can mask a coat-color gene).

### Sex-Linked Example
A colorblind trait is X-linked recessive (*Xᶜ*). Cross a carrier mother (*Xᶜ X⁺*) with a
normal-vision father (*X⁺ Y*):

|      | **X⁺** | **Y** |
|------|--------|-------|
| **Xᶜ** | X⁺Xᶜ | XᶜY |
| **X⁺** | X⁺X⁺ | X⁺Y |

- Daughters: all have normal vision (1 carrier : 1 non-carrier).
- Sons: **1/2 colorblind** (XᶜY) : 1/2 normal (X⁺Y).
This is why X-linked recessive traits typically pass from carrier mothers to sons.

## Pedigrees and Human Genetics

A **pedigree** chart tracks a trait through a family (squares = males, circles = females,
filled = affected). Analyzing pedigrees lets geneticists infer whether a trait is
dominant, recessive, autosomal, or sex-linked, and predict the probability that future
children will be affected.

## Check Your Understanding

1. **Two heterozygous black guinea pigs (Bb) are crossed. What phenotypic ratio is
   expected, and what fraction of the black offspring are heterozygous?**
   *Answer:* *Bb × Bb* gives 1 BB : 2 Bb : 1 bb — a **3 black : 1 white** phenotypic
   ratio. Of the black offspring (BB + 2 Bb = 3 total), **2/3 are heterozygous (Bb)**.

2. **A woman with type AB blood and a man with type O blood have children. What blood
   types are possible?**
   *Answer:* Mother = *I^A I^B*, father = *ii*. Offspring receive *I^A* or *I^B* from mom
   and *i* from dad → *I^A i* (type A) or *I^B i* (type B), a **1 A : 1 B** ratio. No AB or
   O children are possible.

3. **In the dihybrid cross RrYy × RrYy, what is the probability of an offspring that is
   round and green (R_ yy)?**
   *Answer:* Round (R_) = 3/4; green (yy) = 1/4. Multiply: 3/4 × 1/4 = **3/16**.
