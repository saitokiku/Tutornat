# Chapter 2 — Cellular Energetics: Respiration & Photosynthesis

**Concepts:** Cellular Respiration · Photosynthesis · **Unit 3**

## ATP: The Cell's Energy Currency

Cells run on **ATP (adenosine triphosphate)**, a nucleotide with three phosphate groups.
Energy is released when the terminal phosphate bond is hydrolyzed:

`ATP + H₂O → ADP + Pᵢ + energy`

The energy released powers cellular work (muscle contraction, active transport,
biosynthesis). ATP is continually regenerated from ADP and inorganic phosphate. Both
cellular respiration and photosynthesis are strategies for capturing energy and using it
to make ATP. Many of these reactions are **redox reactions**: **oxidation** is loss of
electrons, **reduction** is gain of electrons (remember *OIL RIG*). Electron carriers
such as **NAD⁺/NADH** and **FAD/FADH₂** (respiration) and **NADP⁺/NADPH** (photosynthesis)
shuttle high-energy electrons between reactions.

## Cellular Respiration

Cellular respiration extracts energy from glucose to make ATP. The overall equation is:

`C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + energy (ATP + heat)`

**Aerobic respiration** occurs in three stages plus a linking step.

### 1. Glycolysis (in the cytoplasm)
Glucose (6 carbons) is split into two **pyruvate** molecules (3 carbons each). Glycolysis
does not require oxygen. It has an energy-investment phase (2 ATP used) and an
energy-payoff phase, yielding a **net of 2 ATP** (by substrate-level phosphorylation) and
**2 NADH** per glucose.

### 2. Pyruvate Oxidation (link reaction, mitochondrial matrix)
Each pyruvate is oxidized, loses a CO₂, and is attached to coenzyme A to form
**acetyl-CoA**, producing **1 NADH per pyruvate** (2 NADH per glucose).

### 3. Krebs Cycle / Citric Acid Cycle (mitochondrial matrix)
Acetyl-CoA (2 carbons) joins oxaloacetate (4 carbons) to form citrate (6 carbons). As the
cycle turns twice per glucose, it releases CO₂ and captures energy. **Per glucose**, the
Krebs cycle yields **2 ATP, 6 NADH, and 2 FADH₂**, and releases the remaining carbons as
CO₂.

### 4. Oxidative Phosphorylation (electron transport chain + chemiosmosis, inner mitochondrial membrane)
The NADH and FADH₂ made in earlier steps deliver electrons to the **electron transport
chain (ETC)**. As electrons pass down the chain, protons (H⁺) are pumped into the
intermembrane space, building an electrochemical **gradient**. Protons flow back through
**ATP synthase**, which uses the energy to phosphorylate ADP — a process called
**chemiosmosis**. **Oxygen is the final electron acceptor**, combining with electrons and
protons to form water. Oxidative phosphorylation produces the bulk of the ATP —
approximately **26–28 ATP** per glucose.

**Total yield:** about **30–32 ATP** per glucose molecule under aerobic conditions.

### Without Oxygen: Fermentation
When oxygen is absent, the ETC stops and NADH cannot be reoxidized there. **Fermentation**
regenerates NAD⁺ so glycolysis can continue producing 2 ATP. In **alcoholic fermentation**
(yeast) pyruvate becomes ethanol and CO₂; in **lactic acid fermentation** (muscle cells)
pyruvate becomes lactate. Fermentation makes no additional ATP beyond glycolysis's net 2.

## Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. It occurs in
**chloroplasts** and is, in overall terms, the reverse of respiration:

`6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂`

Photosynthesis has two linked stages: the **light-dependent reactions** (in the
**thylakoid membranes**) and the **Calvin cycle** (in the **stroma**).

### Light-Dependent Reactions
Chlorophyll and accessory pigments in **photosystems II and I** absorb light. In
**photosystem II**, light energy excites electrons and drives the **splitting of water
(photolysis)**, which releases O₂ as a byproduct and replaces lost electrons. Excited
electrons travel down an electron transport chain, pumping protons into the thylakoid
space. As in mitochondria, protons flow back through **ATP synthase** to make **ATP**
(this is **photophosphorylation**). In **photosystem I**, re-energized electrons reduce
**NADP⁺ to NADPH**. The light reactions thus produce **ATP, NADPH, and O₂**.

### The Calvin Cycle (Light-Independent Reactions)
The Calvin cycle uses the ATP and NADPH from the light reactions to build sugar from CO₂;
it does not directly require light. It has three phases:

1. **Carbon fixation:** the enzyme **RuBisCO** attaches CO₂ to the 5-carbon **RuBP**,
   forming an unstable 6-carbon molecule that splits into two 3-carbon **3-PGA**.
2. **Reduction:** ATP and NADPH convert 3-PGA into **G3P** (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate),
   a 3-carbon sugar.
3. **Regeneration:** most G3P is used, with ATP, to regenerate RuBP so the cycle
   continues. For every **3 CO₂** fixed (three turns), **one G3P** exits; two G3P combine
   to form one glucose.

### Labeled Example: Tracing One Carbon
Follow a CO₂ molecule entering a leaf:
1. CO₂ diffuses into the stroma and is fixed by RuBisCO onto RuBP → two 3-PGA.
2. ATP and NADPH (made by the light reactions) reduce 3-PGA to G3P.
3. That carbon, now in G3P, can be assembled with others into glucose. The glucose can
   later be broken down in **respiration**, releasing the carbon again as CO₂ — closing
   the loop between the two processes.

## Comparing the Two Processes

| Feature | Cellular Respiration | Photosynthesis |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Cytoplasm & mitochondria | Chloroplasts |
| Reactants | Glucose + O₂ | CO₂ + H₂O + light |
| Products | CO₂ + H₂O + ATP | Glucose + O₂ |
| Electron carrier | NADH, FADH₂ | NADPH |
| Energy source | Chemical bonds in glucose | Sunlight |

Together these processes cycle carbon and energy through ecosystems: photosynthesis
stores solar energy in sugars, and respiration releases it to power life.

## Check Your Understanding

1. **Why is oxygen essential for the high ATP yield of aerobic respiration?**
   *Answer:* Oxygen is the final electron acceptor of the electron transport chain. It
   pulls electrons through the chain, keeping it running so protons are pumped and ATP
   synthase produces ATP. Without O₂, the ETC backs up, NADH is not reoxidized, and cells
   fall back on fermentation for only 2 ATP per glucose.

2. **How are chemiosmosis in mitochondria and in chloroplasts similar?**
   *Answer:* Both use an electron transport chain to pump H⁺ across a membrane, creating a
   proton gradient. In both, protons flow back through ATP synthase to make ATP. They
   differ in electron source (glucose-derived NADH vs. light-excited electrons from water)
   and location (inner mitochondrial membrane vs. thylakoid membrane).

3. **What do the light reactions provide to the Calvin cycle, and what does the Calvin
   cycle return?**
   *Answer:* The light reactions supply **ATP and NADPH** used to fix and reduce carbon.
   The Calvin cycle returns **ADP, Pᵢ, and NADP⁺** to be re-energized by the light
   reactions, allowing both stages to continue cycling.
