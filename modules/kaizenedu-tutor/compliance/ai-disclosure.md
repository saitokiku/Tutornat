## 1. Where the AI nature is disclosed

Disclosure is not one page; it is four surfaces, and all four have to hold.

| Surface | What it says | Enforced by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every session screen | A persistent "AI tutor" label, always visible, never dismissible | Spec §5.10 A, R10, R28; `PRODUCT.aiLabel` in `kaizen.config.ts` |
| The tutor itself | When a learner asks what it is, it says it is an AI tutor with a character. It never claims to be a person | Spec §5.10 A; the persona prompt rules |
| This page | The full explanation, linked from the footer and from onboarding | R13 |
| The parent direct notice and privacy policy | That replies, grading and the progress report are produced by a language model | `parent-direct-notice.md` |

## 2. What the tutor is

The tutor is an artificial-intelligence system: a language model writes each reply, a speech system
reads it aloud, a speech-recognition system turns the learner's voice into text, and a drawing system
puts steps on the whiteboard. It is shown as a stylised 2D character so a learner can see it listen,
think and talk. It is not photoreal, it is not a person, it is not a recording of a person, and it
does not pretend to be either.

## 3. What it will not do

- It will not claim to be human.
- It will not say it misses a learner, ask them to come back, or use guilt to extend a session. The
  spec is explicit: no "I miss you", no guilt about leaving (§5.10 A).
- It will not lengthen a session to keep a learner on screen. Sessions end at their scheduled length
  regardless of how engaged the learner is (§5.10 C, D15).
- It will not give the final answer to graded-looking work before the learner attempts a step.
- It will not ask a learner for their name, school, address or contacts, and it redirects off-topic
  requests back to studying (§5.6).
- It will not produce sexual, violent or hateful content, or romantic roleplay, and it applies
  stricter rules for the 9–12 band.
- It will not use attention data to re-engage a learner. Attention aggregates adapt the lesson and
  inform the parent; they never trigger a notification to the child or a re-engagement campaign
  (§11.2 item 13).

## 4. Where it can be wrong

Language models make mistakes. An explanation can be misleading. A graded check can be marked
wrongly. A drawing can be off. The tutor checks arithmetic before stating it and says when it is
unsure, but none of that makes it reliable in the way a textbook or a teacher is.

**Treat what it says as a strong first explanation, not as an authority.**

## 5. What it must never be relied on for

This is the operative list. The tutor is not, and must never be presented as, a source of:

- **Any assessment of a child's ability, aptitude or needs.** The progress report is generated by
  software from a handful of check items. It is not a professional evaluation and must never read
  like one.
- **A diagnosis of anything** — a learning disability, ADHD, dyscalculia, dyslexia, or any other
  condition. The tutor does not screen for these and no number it produces means anything about them.
- **A placement, grade-level or eligibility decision**, including anything feeding an IEP, a 504
  plan, or a school placement.
- **Medical, psychological, legal or financial advice.**
- **Crisis support.** If a learner discloses self-harm or abuse, the tutor responds with care,
  provides crisis resources, stops tutoring, and flags the session for the account holder and for
  review — it does not counsel, and it is not a substitute for a person.
- **Supervision.** It is not a childminder and does not know what is happening in the room.
- **A factual authority** for anything a learner will be graded on without checking.

## 6. How mastery is worded, and why

A parent reading "Fractions — 78%" must not read it as a professional judgement. The product's
wording rules (`lib/tutor/report/parent-report.ts`, and law 2 of the strategy integration doc):

- Every number is called an **estimate**: the report renders "on track (estimate)" and "in progress
  (estimate)".
- The word **mastery** is reserved for `confirmed` — an unaided check at least a day later.
- The tutor never grades its own help as mastery; `evidence_events.assisted` records whether help was
  given, and assisted work cannot confirm a skill.

Counsel should read the parent report end to end against this section. If any surface reads as a
human's professional assessment, that surface is wrong, not this page.

## 7. What the AI receives

The models receive the text of the conversation, the problem the learner brought — including the
uploaded image or PDF for the reading stage — and a compact summary of the learner's skills and past
misconceptions. Voice is transcribed to text and the recording is discarded. **No camera image ever
reaches an AI provider, because there is no camera in this product.** The full list of providers and
what each receives is in the privacy policy and in `vendor-data-flow.md`.

We do not use learner data to train models.

## 8. Which models

The operator routes each stage of a session — the live turn, grading, the summary, reading an
uploaded problem — to a specific model, and can change that routing to improve quality, speed or
cost. Learners and parents never configure a provider, and no provider key ever reaches the browser.

> **Draft note.** "Can change that routing" is doing a lot of work in that sentence. Routing is a
> single environment variable (`MODEL_ROUTES`), so a change of provider is a change of recipient
> without a change of notice. See `vendor-data-flow.md` §1 and `open-questions.md` Q5.

## 9. When something is off

Every session has a report button. Use it for an answer that is wrong, a reply that is
inappropriate, or anything that worried you. A report creates a flagged record (`flags`) that a
person reviews.

## 10. For counsel: what is being asked of you here

1. Is this disclosure adequate under the FTC Act's deception standard for a product used by
   children, and under any state AI-disclosure law that applies once the home jurisdiction is fixed?
2. The House-passed KIDS Act carries AI-chatbot safety provisions and is not law (spec §11.3). Should
   we write to that standard now?
3. Is §5's "never rely on this for" list complete, and does it need to appear anywhere other than
   this page — in onboarding, above the parent report, or in the direct notice?
4. Does the character-with-a-face presentation create any disclosure obligation beyond the label —
   for a 4-to-8-year-old who cannot read the label?
