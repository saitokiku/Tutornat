### 1.2 Notes on the table

- Two products in the table are voice-first for children and paid by parents: Synthesis (K-5 math, $29/month) and Ello (reading, $14.99/month). Both sit below the age band Natural Tutor is launching with (13-17 and adults). Amira is voice-first but sold only to schools. Speak is the closest voice-first commercial comparison, but for adults.
- In the 13-17 band the strongest free substitutes shipped in the last 13 months: ChatGPT study mode (July 2025), Gemini Guided Learning (August 2025), and ChatGPT for Teens (August 2026), which routes under-18s automatically into a study-mode-capable product with parental controls (techcrunch.com, 2026-08-18). Khanmigo at $4/month is the priced reference point that parents will compare a $29/month product against.
- Other 2025-2026 entrants reported by a startup-tracking blog: Vimi, iEduGPT, SigIQ.ai, Giant and Pensive raised roughly $7M-$12M seed rounds each, with math described as one of the most durable AI education categories (newmarketpitch.com, 2025-2026; secondary source, amounts not verified against filings).
- "My Math Academy" (Age of Learning, an ESSA-rated early-math game with a kindergarten effect size of 0.16) is a different product from Math Academy and is often conflated with it in search results (evidenceforessa.org, live; files.eric.ed.gov ED662360).
- Chegg is the cautionary case for text-based paid study help: revenue halved in a year as students moved to free chatbots and Google AI Overviews (businesswire.com, 2026-08-06; fueler.io, 2026).

### 2.3 Reading of the evidence

The large positive results (Harvard, Nigeria, Tutor CoPilot, the NUMI mastery arm) share a structure: an adult in the loop or a mandated session, tightly designed pedagogy, and a bounded curriculum. The disappointing results (Khanmigo in the wild, ALEKS post-ChatGPT, plain GPT-4 in Turkey) share the opposite: optional access and an answer-shaped interface. Natural Tutor's design choices (coach mode, checks, no answer dumps) match the guarded arm of Bastani et al., but the Khanmigo trial is a warning that a well-designed tutor students are free to ignore mostly gets ignored. The evidence favors a distribution channel that supplies the session structure and accountability the product cannot supply on its own.

### 3.4 What reviews of AI tutor apps complain about

### 6.2 Session model and assumptions

- 25 minutes wall clock, 12 tutor turns of about 60 spoken words each. Token rule from Anthropic's pricing page: 1 token is about 0.75 words, so 60 words is about 80 output tokens per turn. Characters: about 5.7 per word including spaces, so 60 words is about 342 characters; 12 turns is about 4,100 characters, or about 4.8 minutes of speech at 150 words per minute.
- Learner speaks about 35 words per turn (about 47 tokens); 12 turns is about 420 words, about 2.8 minutes of actual speech; with pauses and endpointing, about 5 minutes of audio sent to ASR when voice-activity detection gates the stream, or 25 minutes if the ASR socket stays open for the whole session.
- System prompt 4,000 tokens sent on every turn, plus the growing transcript. Turn k input = 4,000 + 127 × (k − 1) + 47. Summed over 12 turns: 48,000 + 127 × 66 + 564 = 56,946 input tokens, of which 48,000 are the repeated system prompt (cacheable) and 8,946 are conversation.
- Output: 12 × 80 = 960 tokens. Whiteboard variant: add about 120 tokens of drawing and pointing commands per turn (assumption), so 200 output tokens per turn, 2,400 total, and the history term grows to 247 per turn: 48,000 + 247 × 66 + 564 = 64,866 input tokens.
- One model call per tutor turn. No separate grading, student-model or safety calls are counted (each would add roughly one Flash-Lite-class call per turn, on the order of a cent per session). No retries, no barge-in re-synthesis, no image input from the whiteboard.

## 7. Business-direction assessment

### 7.1 Option A: direct-to-parent subscription as built ($29/month, 3 profiles, 8 pooled hours)

- Who pays: the parent (13-17) or the adult learner; TEFA and other ESA families can pay with public money for "tutoring" and "online educational courses" (comptroller.texas.gov, live), subject to vendor approval processes not researched here.
- Sales cycle: minutes, but the funnel converts at 6.5% trial-to-paid and keeps 14.2% of monthly trial converts at a year in the education category (revenuecat.com, 2026).
- Price points in market: $4 (Khanmigo), $9.99 (Brainly, Photomath), $14.99 (Ello), $29 (Synthesis, Brainly Tutor), $48 (Wild Zebra), $49 (Math Academy); free from ChatGPT for Teens and Gemini Guided Learning. $29 is at the top of the AI band and the bottom of the human band.
- Regulatory burden: moderate now (state companion-chatbot duties in 15 states for the 13-17 band if the persona is in scope, Texas SCOPE and TRAIGA, no COPPA while under-13 stays locked); rising in 2027 (age signals in California, Utah, Louisiana; possible COPPA 2.0).
- What the product would need: a reason a parent pays $29 when the teen already has a free study-mode chatbot. The evidence says the differentiator has to be structure and accountability (scheduled sessions, checks, parent reports) rather than the model, plus retention machinery (weekly parent email, streaks, re-engagement) and a very cheap acquisition channel. Synthesis reached a $10M+ pace in 2025 with a voice-first product, but for ages 5-11, where the child cannot self-serve ChatGPT, and with $17.5M raised and 161 staff (x.com, July 2025; tracxn.com, 2026).
- Founder position: a solo technical founder can ship and iterate this quickly, and the Kaizen community is a near-zero-CAC seed audience; the disadvantage is that paid acquisition at $340 per subscriber (HolonIQ via amraandelma.com, 2026) or $4.70 per install with 6.5% conversion cannot pay back at $29 with education-app retention, so growth is capped by referral.

### 7.2 Option B: tutoring centers, after-school programs and microschools, as the AI tutor students use between human sessions

- Who pays: the center or microschool operator, or the parent through the operator's invoice; in Texas, TEFA funds flow to private schools ($10,474), homeschoolers ($2,000) and tutoring providers, with 100,000 students funded as of 2026-08-13 (comptroller.texas.gov). Six states have universal ESAs (edchoice.org, 2026).
- Sales cycle: weeks. Operators are owner-managers (Mathnasium's 1,047 centers are franchised; the median microschool has 22 students), and the founder ran one of these businesses in Austin.
- Price points seen: parents already pay $140-$200 per subject at Kumon and $300-$450 at Mathnasium per month, so a $15-$30 per student per month between-session add-on is a small fraction of what the operator collects; Amira's $20 per student per year and Khan's $15 per student per year are the institutional reference points, Varsity Tutors' Live+AI shows the "always-on AI between sessions" positioning already exists at the top of the market (investors.nerdy.com, October 2025).
- Regulatory burden: similar to A for 13-17; for under-13 students inside a tutoring center, COPPA still applies (a tutoring business is not a "school" for the school-authorization route unless counsel says otherwise), so the under-13 lock stays until counsel clears it. Operators will ask for FERPA-style data handling and a data-processing agreement; Texas SCOPE and TRAIGA apply either way.
- What the product would need that it does not have: an operator console (roster, assign skills, see session summaries across students), per-center billing, an "assignment from the human tutor" handoff so the AI works on what the human flagged, session scheduling and attendance, and exportable progress for the operator's parent conversations. The pedagogy (fractions to pre-algebra, checks, coach mode) already fits the intervention work centers do.
- Why the evidence points here: every large positive result in Section 2 had an adult and a session structure around the AI (Nigeria's teacher-led sessions, the NUMI mastery workflow, Tutor CoPilot's human tutors), and the large negative result (Khanmigo's 15% engagement) came from optional access. A center supplies the structure; the AI supplies the between-session hours the center cannot staff. No tutoring franchise has shipped this (Section 4.4), and Alpha School's expansion to 50 campuses shows Austin families will pay for AI-centered instruction with human guides (axios.com, 2026-08-02).
- Founder position: strong. The Kaizen club is a live reference customer and a network of operators who trust him; Austin is the ESA and microschool test bed of the moment. The disadvantage is that operator sales, onboarding and support are people work that competes with engineering time, and per-operator revenue is small until a chain or a microschool network signs.

### 7.3 Option C: schools and districts

- Who pays: districts with Title I, state tutoring grants (23 states) or state contracts; ESSER is gone (nssa.stanford.edu, 2025-26).
- Sales cycle: 6-18 months, with 78% of deals over six months and a March-June budget window (smartbrief.com, 2026; civiciq.com, 2026-03-25).
- Price points: $10-$20 per student per year (Khan, Amira); state deals go to incumbents by no-bid contract and still get cut (North Carolina cut Khanmigo from a $10.1M proposal to $500,000 in July 2026).
- Regulatory burden: highest. Vendor privacy agreements, state student-privacy laws, accessibility (WCAG) and procurement paperwork, plus Texas SB 1964 AI disclosure duties on the district side and TEA's thin guidance, which makes districts cautious.
- What the product would need: SSO and rostering (Clever or ClassLink), teacher dashboards, standards alignment reports, an efficacy study (districts now cite the Khanmigo RCT), a privacy-audit trail and a sales motion. Khan gives most of this away at $15 per student and Google and OpenAI give the chatbot away free.
- Founder position: weak. A solo founder cannot run 12-month procurement cycles against Khan Academy, Amira and Google, and the 2026 budget evidence says the buyers are cutting even the incumbents.

### 7.4 Recommendation

Lead with Option B, keep Option A as the billing rail and self-serve entry, and defer Option C.

Concretely: use the twenty beta invites on Kaizen-network operators and TEFA families rather than on anonymous parents; add the operator console and per-student assignment handoff before spending anything on paid acquisition; price the operator offer per active student per month at a small fraction of what the operator charges parents; keep the $29 direct plan live for adults, for 13-17 families arriving through operators, and for ESA-funded homeschoolers, because the provider cost per session ($0.12-$0.24) makes the free 30 minutes and the pooled hours affordable. Revisit Option C only with an operator-generated efficacy dataset in hand and a co-selling partner.

The three facts the recommendation depends on most:

1. Engagement, not model quality, is the binding constraint on unsupervised AI tutoring: the two-year Khanmigo RCT found about 15% regular engagement and gains of 0.06-0.08 SD per year that "resemble Khan Academy practice without AI", while structured, supervised deployments produced 0.31 SD (Nigeria) and measurable mastery gains (NUMI) (nber.org w35620 and w35621, August 2026; chalkbeat.org, 2026-08-25; blogs.worldbank.org, January 2025). If a future study shows unsupervised voice tutors retain teens on their own, Option A strengthens.
2. Consumer subscription economics for education apps are unforgiving next to free substitutes: 6.5% trial conversion and 14.2% one-year retention for monthly trial converts (revenuecat.com, 2026), acquisition at $340 per subscriber for micro-learning apps (HolonIQ via amraandelma.com, 2026), against ChatGPT for Teens (free, 2026-08-18) and Khanmigo at $4. If Natural Tutor's beta shows materially better conversion and retention than these medians, Option A can carry more weight.
3. The operator and ESA channel is large, local and unserved: 100,000 Texas students funded through TEFA by 2026-08-13 with tutoring as an approved expense (comptroller.texas.gov), about 75,000 microschools serving 1.5 million students nationally (microschoolingcenter.org, May 2026), 1,047 Mathnasium and 1,600+ Kumon centers with no between-session AI product (Section 4.4), and district money shrinking (ncnewsline.com, 2026-07-01). If operators turn out to be unwilling to pay or to add a screen between sessions, the recommendation fails and Option A becomes the only path.
