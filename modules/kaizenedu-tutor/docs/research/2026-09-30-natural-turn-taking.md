# Research: natural turn-taking for the voice tutor (2026-09-30)

Produced by a research agent on 2026-09-30. Scope: what makes the tutor "feel like an online call with a live tutor" (pauses, holding a thought, backchannels, not talking over the learner, tolerating slow think-aloud) and the owner's instruction "use Fable and Astra for tutoring and creating drawings and interactive, but use smaller models to talk". ## Summary and recommendation

1. "Astra" in September 2026 is **OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra** (released 2026-09-03/04, $10/$50 per 1M tokens), not Google. Google's Project Astra is a DeepMind prototype folded into the Gemini app; Anthropic has nothing called Astra. `[S]`
2. The owner's sentence is the architecture OpenAI shipped on 2026-09-10: **GPT-Live-1**, a full-duplex voice model at **$0.05/min** that listens while speaking, handles pauses, interruptions and backchannels, and **delegates reasoning and tools to a backend you choose**. `[S]`
3. Anthropic has **no realtime speech API**; the Claude feature list has no audio modality. Fable 5.1 ($10/$50, cache reads $0.25) can only be the thinker behind a talker. `[F]`
4. Gemini 3.8 Live is cheapest ($3/$12 per 1M audio tokens, proactive audio always on) but the Gemini API terms bar services "likely to be accessed by individuals under 18". Blocked for us until counsel clears a Vertex path. `[S]`
5. Recommendation: **browser ⇄ GPT-Live-1 over WebRTC** (ephemeral secret minted by a Vercel route), whiteboard as client-side function tools on the data channel, **Fable 5.1 behind client delegation** for teaching moves and drawings, routed through the browser so Vercel holds no sockets. Today's cascade stays as fallback and safety pre-screen.
6. Time-to-first-audio: today ~2 s; tightened cascade ~0.7–1.0 s; gpt-realtime-2.1 WebRTC ~0.3–0.6 s; GPT-Live-1 similar median with ~5× less jitter (P90 = median + 104 ms). `[S]`
7. Cost: GPT-Live-1 + one Fable delegation per ~90 s ≈ **$0.07 per learner-minute**; gpt-realtime-2.1-mini ≈ $0.05–0.08; tightened cascade ≈ $0.04–0.05. Arithmetic in §4.
8. The "natural" behaviours are mostly policy: `semantic_vad` eagerness `low` (waits up to 8 s), `create_response:false` so the app decides when to answer, out-of-band responses for silent thinking, transcript truncation on barge-in, and backchannel classification so a child's "mhm" never stops the tutor.
9. For a 10-year-old: ASR WER ~12% vs adult ~5–7%; children start turns later and hold the floor with "um"; comprehension drops at fast speech; median oral reading 133 wcpm (grade 4) / 146 (grade 5). Widen patience windows, pace speech ~130–145 wpm, never send notation to a voice.
10. Before building: read the primary GPT-Live docs from a machine that can reach them, and get counsel on OpenAI's under-18 guidance (ZDR required for under-13 data) and Fable's 30-day retention.

**Turn detection** `[S]` https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/realtime-vad · https://hackmd.io/6CTnG7sPSf67BgSZO0udkQ :
- `server_vad`: `threshold` 0.5, `prefix_padding_ms` 300, `silence_duration_ms` 500 ("may jump in on short pauses"), `idle_timeout_ms`, `create_response`, `interrupt_response`.
- `semantic_vad`: a turn model scores "did the user finish" and sets the wait dynamically; `eagerness` `low|medium|high|auto` (auto = medium), **max waits 8 s / 4 s / 2 s**; a trailing "uhhm" lengthens the wait. Developers already ask for a setting *below* `low` `[S]` https://community.openai.com/t/semantic-vad-request-for-additional-eagerness-settings-below-low/1367398 .
- **Hold the reply:** `create_response:false` (VAD still segments and transcribes; the app sends `response.create` when policy allows) or `turn_detection:null` with manual `input_audio_buffer.commit`.
- **Out-of-band responses:** `response.create` with `conversation:"none"` (+ `metadata`, own `tools`) runs a hidden response that neither speaks nor enters history — the primitive for thinking while the learner still talks `[S]` https://community.openai.com/t/realtime-out-of-band-response/1388025 .
- **Text-in/audio-out:** `output_modalities` per session; inject text with `conversation.item.create`, speak with `response.create` `[S]` https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/realtime-mcp .
- **Tools:** function tools run on the client — `response.function_call_arguments.done` arrives on the WebRTC data channel, the browser executes and returns `function_call_output` `[S]` https://webrtchacks.com/the-unofficial-guide-to-openai-realtime-webrtc-api/ ; remote MCP servers are executed by the API (`type:"mcp"`, `server_url`, `allowed_tools`, `require_approval`) `[S]` realtime-mcp guide.
- **Barge-in:** on `input_audio_buffer.speech_started` stop playback and send `conversation.item.truncate` with the milliseconds actually heard (or `output_audio_buffer.clear`); WebRTC/SIP truncate server-side, WebSocket clients must do it. Otherwise history contains words the child never heard `[S]` https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/realtime-conversations · https://zylos.ai/research/2026-07-17-turn-taking-barge-in-realtime-voice-agents/ .
- **Transport:** WebRTC for browsers, WebSocket for servers; `POST /v1/realtime/client_secrets` mints the ephemeral key `[S]` https://apiscout.dev/guides/openai-realtime-api-building-voice-applications-2026 .

## 3. Techniques for the natural behaviours

- **Backchannels.** *Learner's* "mhm" must not stop the tutor: full-duplex models do this natively (GPT-Live-1; Nemotron 93%); in a cascade, pause on onset, classify (agreement / correction / noise), resume from the exact sample if it was only agreement `[S]` https://hamming.ai/resources/voice-agent-interruption-handling-runbook · https://github.com/AashishSinghal/voice-agent-demo . *Tutor's* "mm-hm": Voice Activity Projection backchannel prediction and DualTurn (Interspeech 2026) `[S]` https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.15929 · https://github.com/anyreachai/dualturn ; a drop-in cascade "naturalness layer" exists `[S]` https://github.com/mssharatchandra/duet . Rule: pre-recorded tokens on a separate audio node at low gain during learner speech, never through the TTS queue, never right after a learner pause — fillers "occupy the social slot of a response" `[S]` https://www.parloa.com/labs/insights/the-latency-paradox/ .
- **Thinking while listening.** LiveKit "preemptive generation" is on by default: LLM starts on partial transcripts, discarded if the user continues; TTS waits unless `preemptive_tts` `[S]` https://docs.livekit.io/agents/multimodality/audio/ ; Pipecat tracks the same `[S]` https://github.com/pipecat-ai/pipecat/issues/3321 ; Flux `EagerEndOfTurn` is the STT-side trigger. Research: Voice-Light, LTS-VoiceAgent, RelayS2S `[S]` https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20995 · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.19952 · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.23346 . On OpenAI Realtime: an out-of-band response started on `speech_started`, held, then spoken or dropped.
- **Barge-in with graceful stop.** Truncate to what was heard, clear the queue, keep the tutor's *intent* (which step) in app state and resume ("where was I — the third step"). WebRTC truncates server-side `[S]` https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/realtime-conversations .
- **Filler / latency masking.** Fillers are "the start of the next response"; audible inhalation and rising intonation widen the tolerable pause; GPT-Live-1 does it itself `[S]` parloa and agora above. Ration it with children: "hmm, let me think" every turn is a tell.
- **Prosodic pauses.** Cartesia Sonic-3 inline tags; Google/Azure SSML `<break>`; OpenAI `gpt-4o-mini-tts` style instructions `[S]` https://dev.to/azrty/low-latency-text-to-speech-for-ai-voice-agents-3jal · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/speech-synthesis-markup . On S2S, prompt it ("pause after each question; do not fill the silence").
- **Deciding not to speak.** Gemini's proactive audio and `eagerness:low` help, but full-duplex models "take the floor when asked, not when needed" — poor at *proactive* timing `[S]` https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19596 . The wait must be app policy: after a question, `create_response:false` for a think window (6–10 s at this age, §5), then a soft check-in before any hint.
- **Full-duplex in a browser.** Moshi, Unmute and Nemotron need a GPU server we run plus a WebSocket — not Vercel-hostable. The only hosted full-duplex model with browser WebRTC is GPT-Live-1; Gemini 3.8 Live is near-full-duplex over WebSocket.
- **Attention/gaze.** Gaze-reactive dialogs re-oriented attention and improved learning on deep-reasoning questions (Gaze Tutor) `[S]` https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1071581912000250 ; a 287-student study cut mind-wandering and improved retention `[S]` https://visualcognition.nd.edu/assets/424949/hutt_chi21.pdf . Invariant (c): on-device only; just an attention *state* reaches the voice policy.

## 4. Recommended architecture

**Roles.** Talker: GPT-Live-1. Thinker/drawer: Fable 5.1 via **client delegation** — on each delegation event our server assembles transcript + session state (skill node, misconception tags, last check) and streams a Fable call returning `{say, ops[]}`: a short spoken move plus whiteboard operations in the existing tag grammar. GPT-6 Astra via Responses delegation is the one-line alternative if delegation should stay inside OpenAI (same price class); Sonnet 5.5 for routine moves. Fallback: today's cascade with Flux eager EOT + streaming TTS.

**Whiteboard when the talker is S2S.** (1) Client-side function tools (`draw_fraction_bar`, `write_step`, `highlight`) in the session; arguments arrive on the data channel, the browser renders and returns `function_call_output` — no server hop. (2) Fable's richer drawings ride the delegation result: the browser applies `ops` and hands `say` to GPT-Live, which narrates in its own voice. The tag parser stays; only its input source changes.

**Vercel topology.** Browser ⇄ OpenAI **WebRTC direct**; audio never touches Vercel. Route `/api/voice/token` mints the ephemeral secret with session config (instructions, tools, `turn_detection`, `output_modalities`). Delegation goes **through the browser**: data-channel event → `fetch('/api/tutor/think')` (streaming HTTP to Fable via the existing turn engine) → result posted back on the data channel; no function holds a socket. Vercel WebSockets are in public beta (2026-06-22), pinned to one function for its max duration (800 s GA with Fluid, 1,800 s beta), no cross-instance broadcast `[S]` https://ably.com/vercel/websockets-on-vercel · https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations — fine for an optional sideband observer, wrong for audio; the sibling doc cites a shorter 5/30-min limit, so UNVERIFIED either way. Vercel AI Gateway fronts `gpt-realtime-2`, `gemini-3.8-live` and GPT-Live with short-lived tokens (`gateway.experimental_realtime.getToken`), WebSocket-based; it sends `store:false` but "that alone does not establish zero data retention" `[S]` https://vercel.com/docs/ai-gateway/modalities/realtime · https://vercel.com/docs/ai-gateway/modalities/realtime/gpt-live .

## 5. What changes for a 10-year-old

- **Recognition is worse.** Age-specialised adapters reach ~12.3% WER on ages 3–12 (macro 17.6%); accuracy stabilises after 10 and nears adult parity at 12–13; consumer assistants "continue to underperform on children's speech"; read-alouds transcribe better than free talk and classroom noise hurts children more `[S]` https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05440 · https://pubs.aip.org/asa/jel/article/5/3/035201/3338215 · https://the-learning-agency.com/guides-resources/closing-the-child-speech-recognition-gap-evidence-limitations-and-paths-forward/ . Use keyterm prompting (fraction vocabulary) and let Fable reason over a noisy transcript rather than ask the child to repeat.
- **Patience windows.** Children plan turns more slowly, lose the floor more often, and hold it with "um/uh" — a filler means "still mine", not end of turn `[S]` https://benjamins.com/catalog/tilar.12.09cas · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4464110/ . Starting defaults (to measure, not cited): `semantic_vad` `eagerness:low` or Flux `eot_threshold` 0.8 + `eot_timeout_ms` 6000 during answers; `create_response:false` after every tutor question with a 6–10 s think window; a whispered check-in, not a hint, at its end.
- **Interruption tolerance.** A child's overlap is more often self-talk or agreement than correction: don't cut the tutor on one short vocalisation; require ≥2 recognised words or ≥800 ms of speech to barge in (UNVERIFIED thresholds), and always resume from the truncated point.
- **Math pronunciation.** Never send `3/4`, `x^2` or LaTeX to a voice: spell "three-fourths", "x squared". Cascades can use SSML `<say-as interpret-as="fraction">` or Azure's `<mstts:prompt domain="Math"/>`/MathML `[S]` https://speechgen.io/en/node/fraction/ · https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/speech-synthesis-markup-pronunciation · https://www.readspeaker.com/blog/turning-ssml-to-voice-for-stem/ . Whiteboard gets notation; voice gets words.
- **Reading and listening speed.** Hasbrouck & Tindal 2017 medians: grade 4 spring 133 wcpm, grade 5 146 `[S]` https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED594994.pdf · https://www.readnaturally.com/article/hasbrouck-tindal-oral-reading-fluency-data-2017 . Conversation runs ~150 wpm `[S]` https://virtualspeech.com/blog/average-speaking-rate-words-per-minute ; children comprehend better at slower rates and fast speech hurts `[S]` https://doi.org/10.1044/jshr.1603.367 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24160304 . Pace ~130–145 wpm, ~120 for a new idea; reveal whiteboard text in ≤8-word lines synchronised with speech so the child never reads faster than 133 wcpm while listening.
