# Algebra 1 first, an English spine after math is perfected

Manny, 2026-09-12 16:29 CDT, iMessage, verbatim:

> "For ro-2 I wanna real quick back track and finalize who will will launch for first I want an English/ reading comprehension spine off after we have math perfected. Here's some research I did. Grades 6–9 — with Algebra 1 (gr. 8–9) as the single hottest node, and grade 3 reading as the second.
> The data:
> - Math is ~half of all tutoring sessions; English is #2. Within math, Algebra is the runaway leader — Tutor.com logged ~100,000 algebra sessions in one year, more than double the prior year. Algebra 1 is taken in 8th or 9th grade and is the course that gates the entire HS math sequence, so parent panic concentrates there.
> - 8th grade reading is the worst-performing spot in the country: 33% of 8th graders score below NAEP Basic — the highest share ever recorded. 4th grade reading also declined; 4th grade math was the only bright spot.
> - Grade 3 reading is demand created by law, not by parent choice — a majority of states have third-grade retention statutes tied to a reading test. Deadline + consequence = highest urgency and least price resistance of any elementary segment.
> - High school (10–12) holds the largest revenue share (~40% of global tutoring revenue) — but that's SAT/ACT and college-app driven, not homework help.
> - Parents who buy tutoring report ~$357/month willingness to pay; only 15–20% of students get any tutoring in a given month.
> 
> What you're probably getting wrong — demand is the wrong thing to optimize, three corrections:
> 1. High school has the highest revenue and the worst business. Seasonal, churns hard (the goal completes when the test is taken), demands your most expensive tutors, competing with Princeton Review/Kaplan/Varsity. Elementary is the opposite: lower hourly, cheapest tutor supply, and a 3rd grader can stay enrolled for five years. Kumon's whole model is LTV, not hourly rate.
> 2. Need and willingness-to-pay are not the same curve for English. The deepest comprehension gap is grades 4–7, but that's exactly where parents don't buy — a B in ELA hides a comprehension problem, while a failing Algebra grade is undeniable. English sells at two ends only: phonics/reading-level in K–3 (fear of retention) and essay/writing in 9–12 (fear of college). Grades 4–7 English is a real need with a broken sales trigger. Don't build there first unless you're bundling it into a math sale.
> 3. The buyer is the parent, and parents buy at gates, not grades. Sell "get through the 3rd-grade reading test," "don't fall behind before Algebra," "Algebra 1 recovery." Positioning by grade level ("6th grade tutoring") converts far worse than positioning by the specific thing the parent is afraid of."

## What this decides and what it opens
- **Sequence:** math first, then an English/reading-comprehension spine — *"after we have math perfected."*
- **His three corrections are the durable part**, and they are strategy, not data: revenue share is not
  business quality (high school churns by design because the goal completes); need and willingness to
  pay diverge for English in grades 4–7 because a B hides the problem while a failing algebra grade
  cannot be hidden; and **parents buy at gates, not grades** — position on the specific fear, never on
  a grade level.
- **It sharpens the wedge rather than replacing it.** [202609121555-manny-homework-is-the-hook-understanding-is-the-mission](202609121555-manny-homework-is-the-hook-understanding-is-the-mission.md)
  said sell into the demand that exists and repair underneath; this names the demand precisely:
  Algebra 1 panic. K1's conditional answer (algebra readiness) is the *repair*; Algebra 1 is the *sale*.
- **It collides with ADR-0034's 13+ boundary.** Grades 8–9 are ages 13–15 and inside it; "grades 6–9"
  reaches 11–12 and grade 3 reading is 8–9 — both under 13, where COPPA applies and SPEC 4 parks the
  work as P2 behind counsel. Put to him as A) Algebra 1 grades 8–9 only B) grades 6–9, take on COPPA
  now C) A now, grade 3 reading as a separate under-13 product later. PM recommends C. Blocking; no
  default, because proceeding on an assumption here has legal consequences.

## The five figures are claims, not facts, until checked
Tutor.com's ~100,000 algebra sessions and the doubling; NAEP's 33% of 8th graders below Basic and the
4th-grade movement; the count of states with third-grade retention statutes; the ~40% high-school share
of global tutoring revenue; the ~$357/month willingness to pay and the 15–20% participation rate. Each
goes to primary sources before it reaches a plan. This is not distrust of his research — it is the same
rule that caught the 0.44/0.08 pair being spliced from two versions of one paper on the same afternoon.
