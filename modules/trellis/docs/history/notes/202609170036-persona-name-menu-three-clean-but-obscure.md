# The persona-name menu

Astra, brand-naming skill + RO-12 screening discipline, 2026-09-17 00:18–00:34 CDT, 16 minutes
in a 20-minute box. 12 generated, 8 fully screened: 33 USPTO queries, 41 TSDR checks, 8 RDAP.

**The pattern that matters:** every *familiar* first name of a scientist a US parent would
recognise is already an AI persona somewhere — Emmy (a family AI), Nettie (a wellness GPT), Percy
(a children's speech-practice AI), Salim (a health companion) — and Nikola and Rosalind fell the
same way earlier tonight. Fame is exactly what makes a name taken. What survives the screen is
distinctive and, to an American ear, unfamiliar.

**Top three, 0/0/0 live federal hits, no persona found:**
- **Dilhan** — Dilhan Eryurt, Turkish astrophysicist who worked on how the young Sun burned and
  taught at METU for decades. Kit score 8.0.
- **Janaki** — Janaki Ammal, Indian botanist whose chromosome atlas other scientists learned from.
  7.9.
- **Satyen** — Satyendra Nath Bose, who learned the hardest physics so he could teach it. 7.7.

All three are "language conditional": pronunciation on first read and Spanish-language checks
were not done. That is the honest gap, not a formality — a tutor name a six-year-old cannot say is
not a tutor name.

**Reserves with a catch:** Asima (live scientific-services mark), **Lonnie** Johnson (9/8/9 on
clarity/memory/sound — the warmest, easiest name on the list — but education-name neighbours and
an honoree with his own STEM ventures), Inge, Lise (Meitner — the strongest fair-credit story, but
a near-name AI mark and a hard first read).

What this settles: nothing without Manny. It is his taste, and it is the first thing a child will
say to the product. Goes to him as a lettered menu, with the door to counsel on Nikola/Rosalind
left open ([202609170019-nikola-and-rosalind-both-conflict-found-as-persona-names](202609170019-nikola-and-rosalind-both-conflict-found-as-persona-names.md)).
