# Seven corrections and one rule that change the Kaizen plan

Research worker (Opus, 2026-09-11) resolved 25 marked figures in Kaizen-AI `docs/STRATEGY.md` v0.2 against primary sources; seven were wrong. PM spot-checked three verbatim in the saved statute text. Full digest: [RESEARCH](../../research/README-digest.md).

- **Texas price parity is statute** (Educ. Code §29.365(a)–(b)): a TEFA vendor may not charge a participant above its standard price and may not rebate, refund or credit program money. No discounts, no referral credits.
- **TEFA tutor bar is low but specific** (34 TAC §16.404(i)): no certificate, no degree; a national criminal history review within 30 days plus one of three pathways, the widest undefined.
- Public-school students **may** hold TEFA until ADA enrollment; payment within **10 business days**.
- **§529 tutoring** needs a non-relative tutor who is licensed, has taught at an eligible institution, or is a subject expert. **§25F** (2027) routes through §530(b)(3)(A): tutoring with no credential test; Texas opted in.
- Effect sizes overstated; **virtual 0.08 SD vs 0.44 in person**. Accelerate measures months per $1,000, not cost per SD.
- **Seat math**: a $550 seat exhausts a $2,000 award in 3.6 months; the 2026–27 cohort (85,344 funded, 80% under 200% FPL) cannot buy it.
- **Florida PEP** is the only large ESA a public-school child can spend on tutoring (140,000 seats, $7,477–$12,217).
- Incumbents verify nothing externally (21 of 25; six FDDs silent on NWEA, randomized, third-party). Unsupervised access goes unused (Nerdy, Paper).
- Two Texas openings with unresolved process: TEA high-impact tutoring provider approval (§28.0211(a-15)) and the $400 third-grade grant paid direct to providers.
