Q1 | Which parent problem deserves our entire first release, even if we turn other families away?  
Options: A) Missing math foundations blocking algebra (recommended) B) Struggling in Algebra 1 itself C) Reading and writing gaps  
Why it matters: K1 determines curriculum, assessment and recruitment; readiness gives us a bounded problem to solve deeply.  
What a weak answer looks like: “All eventually”—we need the first problem worth excluding others for.  
Follow-up if he picks A: Would you accept rebuilding fractions first even when the parent demands help with tonight’s algebra?  
Follow-up if he picks B: Should we reject learners missing prerequisites or include a remedial path?  
Follow-up if he picks C: Which comes first: understanding a passage or constructing an argument?

Q2 | If familiar teaching methods beat our new mechanic on learning and repeat use, what should we do?  
Options: A) Ship the winner and keep experimenting (recommended) B) Delay six weeks for another mechanic test C) Reject a conventional product  
Why it matters: Separates a company committed to better learning from a research program committed to invention.  
What a weak answer looks like: “We’ll invent something better” without a test or an endpoint.  
Follow-up if he picks A: What advantage must still justify paying us once another tutor copies the method?  
Follow-up if he picks B: What single teaching behavior would your next experiment change?  
Follow-up if he picks C: What observable difference in the learner would make a mechanic new enough?

Q3 | What human coverage will you commit to before the national teen beta opens?  
Options: A) Scheduled session hours with a named backup (recommended) B) Fund a rota for broader hours C) Delay beta until an operator is in place  
Why it matters: Determines service hours, admission controls, staffing costs and whether one person becomes the operating bottleneck.  
What a weak answer looks like: “The agents will handle it” without a human responsible when escalation fails.  
Follow-up if he picks A: Which weekly coverage hours can you personally protect?  
Follow-up if he picks B: What monthly coverage budget will you commit?  
Follow-up if he picks C: What deadline triggers a review if we still have no operator?

Q4 | After two beta redesigns, if teens learn but still need daily chasing, what should happen to this product?  
Options: A) Narrow to teens who want the help (recommended) B) Make parent supervision an explicit requirement C) End this product

What a weak answer looks like: “Make it more engaging” with no limit on retries or parental effort.  
Follow-up if he picks A: Which motivation should we target next: catching up, an upcoming exam or getting ahead?  
Follow-up if he picks B: What weekly parent workload would you be willing to advertise?  
Follow-up if he picks C: Would you stop this tutor or pause the whole Kaizen program?
