### Why the persona distinction matters

A character name can merely identify a character, or it can also identify the source of services. USPTO treats that as a use-dependent distinction. A restrained in-product introduction may present differently from an app listing, paid offering, or landing page called “Ask Rosalind”; calling something a persona does not itself answer the question. This is a screening inference from [TMEP §1301.02(b), names of characters](https://tmep.uspto.gov/RDMS/TMEP/print?href=TMEP-1300d1e10.html&version=current). The actual interface, marketing, audience and services would need review. Similarity and related goods/services matter; sharing a class alone does not prove conflict, and different class numbers do not establish safety. [USPTO likelihood-of-confusion guidance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/likelihood-confusion).

## Two stories a parent or child could be told

**Nikola.** Our tutor’s name honors Nikola Tesla, an inventor who explored how electricity could make things move. He developed a motor that used a turning magnetic field, and other engineers helped turn his idea into a useful machine. Nikola reminds us that a good question can become an experiment, and an experiment can become something that helps people. When a problem feels difficult, we can look closely, try an idea, and learn from what happens. Historical basis: [Smithsonian’s induction-motor record](https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_739995); the final sentences express the proposed tutor’s values.

**Rosalind.** Our tutor’s name honors Rosalind Franklin, a scientist who used careful experiments to explore things too small to see. Her student Raymond Gosling made an X-ray image called Photo 51 under her supervision; their work helped scientists understand DNA’s twisting shape. Rosalind reminds us to notice details, look for evidence, and give everyone credit for their work. We do not have to guess quickly: we can ask a good question and discover the answer one clue at a time. Historical basis: [King’s College London, the story behind Photo 51](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/the-story-behind-photograph-51) and [King’s research history](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/randall/history); the final sentences express the proposed tutor’s values.

Historical wording rule: Franklin **helped reveal DNA’s structure**; she did not discover DNA itself. Credit Gosling for Photo 51. Likewise, do not say Tesla invented electricity or worked alone.
