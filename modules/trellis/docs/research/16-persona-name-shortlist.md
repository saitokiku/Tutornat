## Naming strategy and the kit's scores

Applied the supplied [brand-naming skill](/Users/mann/pm/skills/brand-naming/SKILL.md): strategy → generation → evaluation table → recommendation, with its seven tests (Sound, Memory, Search, Story, Logo, Expansion, Trust). The brief supplied the necessary brand context. Its requested eight-name table and top-three stories take precedence over the kit's default top-five presentation.

**One line:** a warm, exact tutor character who helps household members with learning across subjects and ages. Kaizen Academy LLC is the entity; the company brand remains unresolved. **Audience:** children first, with parents and older learners able to take the same character seriously. **Positioning:** patient explanation now; honest evidence of independent learning later, without making an outcome promise in the name. **Emotional territory:** curiosity, competence, welcome, fair credit. **Three generation directions:** overlooked contributors; teachers who shared difficult knowledge; investigators and inventors with a concrete discovery story. **Avoid:** company-style compounds, household commercial associations, existing assistants, mythologized solo discoveries and pronunciation that requires coaching.

Scores use the kit's **Clarity / Memory / Sound / Visual / Risk / Overall** columns. Numeric scores are editorial judgments out of ten, not measured child responses. Overall is the kit's holistic recommendation score; the kit specifies no arithmetic formula, so no new weighted scoring system was introduced. “Clarity” concerns recognition as a personal character name, not whether the name literally says “education.” Risk combines the observed collision and usability issues; no name receives Low risk while language checks remain unresolved.

Endpoint: `POST https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/prod-stage-v1-0-0/tmsearch`. ## Top three — stories to tell a child

### 1. Dilhan

Dilhan Eryurt used mathematics to ask what the Sun was like when it was very young. She and another scientist, A. G. W. Cameron, built models showing how the young Sun could be much bigger and brighter while it was shrinking. Later, she helped establish astrophysics at a university in Turkey, giving other people a place to study the stars. We would name the tutor Dilhan to remember someone who investigated questions we cannot visit directly—and helped a new generation learn how to ask them. [NASA's original paper](https://www.giss.nasa.gov/pubs/abs/ez01000b.html), [METU account](https://mezun.metu.edu.tr/en/newsletter/).

Story guardrail: the original paper says it confirmed Hayashi's prediction. Do not turn this into a lone-discoverer story or repeat the claim that she was NASA's only woman.

### 2. Janaki

Janaki Ammal studied plants by looking carefully at their cells and chromosomes, the structures that carry inherited information. Her work took her from India to research gardens and universities abroad. With another scientist, C. D. Darlington, she made an atlas of the chromosomes of cultivated plants, giving other researchers a reference they could use. We would name the tutor Janaki because her story shows how patient observation can become knowledge that helps the next person learn something new. [John Innes Centre biography](https://jic.ac.uk/training-careers/postgraduate-opportunities/janaki-ammal-scholarships/).

### 3. Satyen

Satyen Bose wanted his students to understand the new ideas changing physics, even when the books and journals were hard to find in India. He and his colleague Meghnad Saha studied those ideas themselves and worked together to translate Einstein's papers into English. Learning something difficult was also a way to open it up for someone else. We would name the tutor Satyen to remember a scientist who treated teaching as serious work: first understand an idea, then help another person understand it. [S. N. Bose National Centre archive, printed page 26](https://www2.bose.res.in/Prof.S.N.Bose-Archive/objects/Life%2C%20Lectures%20and%20Addresses%2C%20Miscellaneous%20Pieces.pdf).

Satyen is documented historical usage, not a newly invented nickname. The character would use the first name; **Bose** is not proposed as branding. No claim that Einstein stole his work is needed or made.
