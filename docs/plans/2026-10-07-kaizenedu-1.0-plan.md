# KaizenEDU 1.0 — the plan for the final K–9 product

2026-10-07. Supersedes [2026-10-07-real-product-plan.md](2026-10-07-real-product-plan.md) (kept for the
gap review). Owner's brief, in order, today: the dashboard · the ultimate tutor · the shadow worker · a
visual dashboard that makes academic life easy · learning any custom topic creates a course · the magic
box sorts homework, assignments and coursework · a chatbot to talk about topics and tutor · calendar and
course organization so a learner sees their path · deterministic knowledge from real sources · more
interactive · "one record and one mechanism underneath, many faces on top" · K–9 first · "be ready for a
first production build to impress me, but the philosophy should be what you know."

## 0. The philosophy, restated once

1. **Show it, let them touch it, then check it.** Text is the backup channel.
2. **Code checks answers; AI talks.** Nothing a model says becomes a fact in the record.
3. **Practice is not proof.** On your own · with help · not yet · proved later, on different days.
4. **One next step on every screen.** The shadow work is ours, not the family's.
5. **Parents are partners with real information** — never scores, never surveillance.
6. **Real sources, named and linked.** We never copy, never invent a citation.
7. **No engagement tricks.** No streaks to lose, points, leaderboards or "come back" nudges to children.
8. **No AI slop.** Plain, warm, specific. Everything AI-made says so.
9. **Safety before any model.** A crisis gets a person's words, not a model's, and the family is told.
10. **Works without AI.** AI makes it better; it is never the floor.
11. **Natural to use.** A child stays because the work is worth doing, not because anything makes them
    stay; a parent is present with minimal expectations — set up once, glance weekly, act only when
    something matters (§2.10).
12. **A living learning history, not a log.** The record is each child's verified education *and* how
    they learn and what has worked for them; every teaching act states what it intends and records
    whether it happened, so the teaching gets better for this child and, in aggregate and
    de-identified, for everyone (§3.2–3.3).

## 1. Positioning — three doors, one engine

The front of the product asks three questions, and each is a door into the same thing:

| Door | The ask | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Stuck on homework right now?** | photo or text into the magic box | the tutor works it through with them — asks, hints, shows a similar problem worked out; never does it for them |
| **A test coming?** | "I have a test on fractions Friday" | it lands on the calendar; prep sets land on the plan for the three days before; the tutor and the parent can see which skills are proved and which aren't |
| **Need to keep up in general?** | setup once | the daily plan, the path and the record do the shadow work; the parent glances at a weekly email |

The reason to believe, in the owner's words: "we aren't just a Claude and rich text." It is
code-checked practice + real, cited knowledge + an AI tutor with tools + a visual stage + voice, all
reading and writing **one living record** — the child's verified education, their learning history,
and how to teach them — connected so the learner never re-explains anything and feels frictionless:
**growth mode**, kept on by the shadow worker rather than by the family's willpower, and checked
against what we intended so it keeps getting better.

Copy rule (claims discipline): the landing promises only what a visitor can do that day. "Narrated,
touchable lessons" and "a tutor that talks" go up when the voice vendor is live; "video" only when
real video exists (1.1).

## 1.2 The bar: beat tutor.com, Khan Academy, Kumon and Canvas — at once, for a family

"Beat" means: for the job a family hires each of them for, KaizenEDU does it better *and* the four jobs
live in one place. Measured in the pilot by asking families what they used before and what they kept.

| They are best at | We must match | Where 1.0 beats them | What we concede (until) |

The combined claim, and the only one we make on the landing once true: *one place that helps tonight,
runs daily practice, keeps school organized and proves what stuck — for less than one hour of a tutor

## 1.1 One record, one engine, many faces

```
                   Today · Practice · Talk · Learn · Calendar · Family · Growth · Records
                          (faces — each shows a slice of the same record)
   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   Planner      Mastery law     Intake         Course builder     Tutor        Knowledge
   (what next)  (what counts)   (what is it)   (make a course)    (talk+tools) (real facts)
                 Learner model (verified education · teaching profile)  ·  Outcomes loop
   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                      THE RECORD — accounts · learners · attempts (append-only, with
                      misconception tags) · sets · teaching acts (intent → outcome) · school
                      items · classes · teacher notes · school results · plan marks · reading ·
                      tutor threads · courses · notes · teaching-profile edits   (Postgres, synced)
```

Everything a face shows is computed from the record by pure functions (`learning/`, `planner/`,
`practice/`). Everything a face changes goes through `lib/*` actions. That is why one record can
serve a kindergartner, a 7th grader, a homeschool parent and a parent at 8 pm with a worksheet.

## 2. The product, face by face (the 1.0 state)

Each face lists: what you see · what you can do · what is behind it · K–2 difference.

### 2.1 Today — the dashboard

- **See:** greeting and date; a status strip (due today · test in N days · checks ready · minutes
  left in today's budget); the one raised **Next** card; the rest of today's plan as rows with done
  marks; **Coming up at school** (7 days); **Courses in progress** (continue points); the **magic box**.
- **Do:** start the next thing in one tap; mark school work done; open Get help (tutor with the item);

- **Behind:** `planFor()` (checks → prep → due → teacher notes → daily sets → reviews → lesson),

### 2.2 The magic box — universal intake (new)

- **See:** one box: "What's going on? Learn something, homework, a test, or practice." Chips for
  common asks. A classifier shows its guess as a small choice row before anything happens.
- **Do:** type or paste; drop a photo/PDF/worksheet; pick what it is if the guess is wrong.
- **Behind** (`lib/intake.ts`): rules first (dates → school item; "test/quiz/exam" → test;
  "homework/worksheet/due/page/pp." → homework; "practice/drill/more of" → practice; "learn/why/how/

  - **Test / quiz** → a school item; prep sets appear on the plan for the 3 days before; the item page
    shows which linked skills are proved / practicing / not started.
  - **Practice X** → a set on the matching skill, or AI questions for an open topic (labelled).
  - **Learn X** → the course builder (2.5).
- **K–2:** the grown-up uses it; the child's version is picture tiles.

### 2.3 Practice — the at-home Kumon

### 2.10 The learning loop and what keeps a learner coming back

> **Owner update (2026-10-07), supersedes the "no streaks, points, badges" rule below:** "nothing wrong with
> gamed engagement if its for learning and user has communicated the north star neither are we leading them
> anywhere else nor gaming for purpose, its a tool to make their life and learning better and fun" — and
> "a super robust learning workflow … a accountability real life parent or teacher or streaks with your
> friends something like that to keep you engaged". Test for every mechanic: it serves the goal the learner
> (and family) set, it never fakes progress, and it never leads them anywhere else. And the limit, same day:
> "dont overdo engagement mechanics, those should only be there if justified to work, the product should be
> a clean teacher replacement" — so a mechanic ships only with evidence that it improves learning or
> showing up, in the smallest form that works; the default is a clean, calm teacher. This section is the result: an evidence panel (research, three designs, three judges) revised to these rules and independently checked, 2026-10-07.

> This replaces the previous 2.10 and extends §3. Three designs went to a panel of three judges, and each design won one judge. The **learning engine** comes from *Edge and Wonder*: placement, accommodations, the proof moment and loop-stage tags. The **sitting script and the week** come from *Kaizen Rhythm*. The **people layer, the legal discipline and the code baselines** come from *The Circle and the Week*. Where they disagreed, the decision is in I. The engagement rules were then revised against the owner's three instructions of 2026-10-07; the test below is how.

**The idea in one paragraph.** Software supplies the dose, the timing and an honest record. People supply the relationship and the accountability. What makes a child better at something is the loop inside each sitting:
1. an example first;
2. guided practice that mostly succeeds;
3. recall on later days, mixed with skills that are easy to confuse;
4. a delayed check done alone on new problems;
5. spaced reviews.

The weekly rhythm exists to make that loop happen on most days. A person who notices is what makes it matter. What best predicts a child's own motivation is feeling capable, then having choices that are theirs, then someone who notices. In other words: competence, then autonomy, then relatedness (Howard 2021; Bureau 2022; large meta-analyses, mostly correlational). The honest aim is +0.15 to 0.30 SD on an outside measure over a semester, never "2 sigma" (Nickow 2020; Kraft 2024; von Hippel 2024). Habit effects are judged at week 8 or later (Lally 2010, median 66 days; Rodrigues 2022 on novelty).

**The test every engagement mechanic passes.** This is the owner's three instructions, read together.

| | A mechanic is in only when | It fails when |
|---|---|---|
| a | it serves the goal the learner or family set | it pushes a goal nobody chose, like more minutes or more opens |
| b | evidence says it helps learning or showing up **at this age** | the only support is adult retention data or a company blog |
| c | it is in its smallest effective form | a smaller version would do the same job |
| d | it never fakes progress and never leads anywhere else | activity shows as achievement, or it points at anything but the learning |

Game-like is fine; unjustified is not.
- A mechanic that fails the test is **not built**. It can come back with evidence for that age band and a kill rule.
- A mechanic is **never** built only where evidence shows harm to children or the law forbids it. Each of those cites which (E2).
- With every optional piece switched off, the product is a calm, clean teacher: a sitting, a proof, a week of dots and one weekly note to the grown-up.

|---|---|---|
| "super robust learning workflow" | The sitting loop and the proof (A, B) | Step-by-step entry for multi-step math, 1.1. Step-checked tutors reach d ≈ .76 vs .31 for answer-only checking (VanLehn 2011), about 0.2 SD at scale (Pane 2014) |
| "human … parent or teacher" | Friday note, teach-back, "Our week", teacher share link after counsel | Teacher seat; human-tutor add-on |
| "extra sensors" | Task signals, ink, read-aloud, paper photos; voice answers in M6 | K–3 listening to children read aloud; handwriting recognition in answer fields |

Also the family circle, and for ages 13–15 a study buddy and a friends circle once accounts run on the server and counsel confirms the 13+ split (D) | A friends circle for under-13s after the consent gate in D, 1.1 at the earliest. A friend streak that can break is not built (E2) |

**§0 principle changes.**
- **Rewrite 7: Engagement earns its place.** A mechanic ships only if it passes the four-part test above.
  - Game-like is fine. A mechanic is banned only for harm to children or the law (E2).
  - The week is a forgiving record the family chose, with rest days built in. A child can't lose, buy or break anything.
  - A mechanic stays only while the improvement loop shows it moves showing up or what the child remembers (first-review survival).
- **Add 13:** People give relationship and accountability; software gives dosage, timing and honesty.

#### B. Proof: the mastery law, numbers unchanged

- **Check days are short days.** A check day is the warm-up (2 items; 1 for K–2), the check and the finish.
- **The check:** 5 fresh top-level items, at least one in a new context.
  - No tutor, photo help, Spotlight or worked steps (`guardSpots`), and no feedback per item.
  - Misses are reviewed at the end and again in tomorrow's warm-up.
  - Optional "sure / not sure" for grade 3 and up. Hiding the tab pauses timing.
- **Void:** 2 or more rapid answers make the check void, neither a pass nor a fail. It reopens tomorrow. A grown-up switch turns voiding off.
- **After a fail:** readiness counts again only after a *different* teaching act on that skill: a worked example, another representation, the tutor on the tagged misconception, or a teach-back. When a skill has no alternative representation, a tutor turn or a teach-back counts (Kulik 1990 vs Slavin 1987).
- **Stuck** (3 sets under 60% right on own, as today): the teaching act changes first, and the grown-up gets a nudge. After 2 more stuck sets the skill is **"coming back to it"**. It is parked for 10 days while `nextSkill` moves to a later skill whose prerequisites are secure, and then it resurfaces (Beck & Gong 2013).
- **The proof moment** (second pass, at least 6 days later, on a different day):
  - One still screen: "Proved: 2-digit subtraction. On your own, on two days a week apart, on problems you hadn't seen." The skill's mark fills in once; with reduced motion it simply appears.
  - A **then-and-now** line from the record: "Sept 12: needed the first step on 6 of 10. Today: 5 of 5 alone."
  - No confetti or sound (not built, E2). The grown-up gets one line and a printable proof page.
- **Proof book** (Growth): dated. It shows "still strong" after each passed review, and "needs a refresh" when that is true ("even strong skills fade; a quick refresh brings it back"). Proved skills are the only achievement marker anywhere (Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999; Soderstrom & Bjork 2015).

**Anchor and if-then plan** (a new "Your time" step in Setup §2.9):
- It records after what, where, which days, and bedtime.
  - K–2: the grown-up picks, and it is phrased to them ("After snack, open KaizenEDU with Maya").
  - Grades 3–6: the child picks one of 3 cues.
  - Grades 7–9: the learner writes their own plan plus one obstacle plan ("If I'm wiped out, I do the tiny sitting").
- It shows on Today and replaces reminders. A sitting at any time of day still counts. An anchor can't be set in the hour before bedtime. It is revisited in "Our week" and offered for re-setup at each term start.
- Evidence: Breitwieser & Reinelt 2026 g = .31, stronger in younger children and ADHD; Patall, Cooper & Robinson 2008 (rules and routines are the strongest form of homework involvement); Beshears 2021 (flexible timing beats a fixed slot). Expect a small effect: Kizilcec 2020 found effects about 10× smaller at scale.

**Quiet hours** (under-13s):
- Bedtime defaults to 20:30 for K–5 and 21:30 for grades 6–9. The grown-up can move it, for example for shift work.
- No new sitting is offered in the hour before bedtime.
- No message goes to a grown-up between 21:00 and 07:00.
- The "best time" fact never falls within 2 hours of bedtime.
- Guardrail: child use after 21:00 = 0.
- Evidence: Carter 2016; Mazza 2016; Peiffer 2020.

**Test prep** (`planner/plan.ts`):
- A test known 7 or more days out gets at least 3 prep sittings on separate days, starting 7–10 days before, with nights in between. The day before is a short mixed recall set, never a cram.
- Under 7 days: every rhythm day left, at most one prep sitting a day.
- `PLAN_RULES.prepDays: 3` becomes `prepWindow: 10, prepSessions: 3`. `NUDGE_RULES.prepDays` stays at 3, decoupled from the plan.
- Evidence: Cepeda 2008; Latimier 2021.

#### D. Streaks with friends, in the form the evidence supports

The owner asked for "streaks with your friends". The evidence supports the social part in one shape: a cooperative goal.
- **For that shape:**
  - Cooperative goals beat competitive and individual ones, on achievement and on peer relationships, at ages 12–15 (Roseth 2008; 148 studies). That is classroom cooperative learning. The panel found no trial of an app friends circle at any K–9 age, so this support is indirect and the kill rule below decides.
  - Progress that is reported to someone works better (Harkin 2016, mostly adults).
  - The individual week underneath has the one child trial: weekly-streak messages raised showing up in grades 4–6, with no discouragement after breaks (Aulagnon 2025; C gives its limits).
- **Against the familiar shape**, a shared count that breaks when one friend misses:
  - Duolingo's +22% for Friend Streaks is correlational and self-selected (Shuttleworth 2024).
  - The EU minors guidelines ask for communication streaks to be off by default (DSA Art. 28, 2025).
  - Snapchat-streak use goes with fear of missing out and problem phone use at about 13 (Belgian survey 2023; weak).
- **The law sets the timing.** Under 13, letting a child share personal information with other children counts as collecting it, and it needs verifiable parental consent first (FTC COPPA FAQ). Circles disclose to other families, so the consent method must be one the COPPA Rule accepts for disclosure; "email plus" is not enough. "Approved contacts only" has to hold in every group path (FTC, Messenger Kids 2023).
- **Age comes from the birthdate the grown-up entered, not the grade.** A circle with any member under 13 follows the under-13 column.

So the form is: your own forgiving week, plus a small circle your grown-up approved, filling one pooled goal that no one's quiet week can break.
- The circle never asks a child for more than the target they chose (a).
- It has no feed, no directory and nothing to browse (d).

Nothing can break in any band. The circle starts fresh each Monday, children see only this week, and a week short of the goal simply ends.

**How a circle starts.**
- A grown-up creates a circle and gets a one-time code. They pass it to other grown-ups themselves, by text or in person. There is no in-app directory, search or "people you may know".
- The receiving grown-up enters the code and picks which child joins. Under 13, they also give consent with the `circle` scope. A child can't invite, join or accept.
- Either family can leave at any time, and leaving wipes that family's rows. A circle that drops below 3 families pauses and shows nothing until a third family joins or it closes.

**Copy rules.** These are exact. They cover every child-facing week and circle surface, in EN and ES, and are enforced by e2e tests and tutor evals.
- **The words we use:** "Your week" · "3 of 4 days" · "That's your week." · "Fresh week." · "Rest week" · "Tiny sitting: 3 minutes, still counts" · "Our circle: 9 of 12 days" · "Circle week done." · "The goal is everyone's targets added up. Rest weeks come off." · "We both did our week." · "Sit together?".
- **Never on these surfaces:**
  - In English: lose, lost, miss, missed, break, broken, behind, reset, start over, in a row, streak, "don't let … down", "only you", "everyone else", "hurry", "N days left".
  - In Spanish: *perder, perdiste, faltaste, romper, roto, atrasado, empezar de cero, seguidos, racha*.
  - Never a name or nickname next to a number, and never a line about who hasn't practised.
  - "Streak" is out because the week does not count in a row, so calling it a streak would be inaccurate (d).
  - Loss words are out for the same reason: nothing on these surfaces can be lost, so they would be untrue (d).
- **The tutor** never gives the week, the circle or a buddy as a reason to practise ("your circle is counting on you"). This is an eval.
- **Grown-ups** get plain numbers ("circle: 9 of 12 days", "target met 7 of 9 weeks"), but never another child's data.
- Spanish strings go through the native reviewer under the same rules.

**Kill rule for circles** (written now, enforced once a few hundred learners are in circles).
- At week 8, compare each member's returns without prompting with their own 4 weeks before joining, and with matched non-members who had similar weeks before. Families who join self-select, the same flaw as Duolingo's +22%. Also look at the leave rate and at the friction answers of circle families.
- Cut circles if returns rise by less than 5 points over the matched non-members, or if friction "a lot" or leaving rises.
- The pilot judges harms only.

**The tutor is continuity, not affection.**
- It opens with the child's own plan and one concrete fact from the record ("Last time the number line helped with 3/4").
- It names the strategy that worked instead of praising.
- It never claims to miss or need the child.
- It hands emotional moments to the grown-up (`note_for_grownup`) and points the child toward people ("Show your grown-up #4").
- All of this is covered by evals.

**Fix my mistake** (grades 3–8): the tutor shows a worked problem that contains the child's most frequent tagged misconception, and the child finds the wrong step. K–5 tap the step; 6–8 say why. It is scripted from the tags, so it works without AI, and it counts as practice only. Its evidence is incorrect worked examples (Barbieri 2023). The protégé effect (Chase 2009; Kobayashi 2019) needs someone to teach, so it supports the teach-back, not this.

**Wonder list:**
- Non-homework questions the child asks in Talk or the magic box are saved. One tap builds a **source-built** course.
- Every under-13 wonder needs a grown-up's approval before it becomes a course, and the safety screen runs first.
- AI-built wonder courses wait for M5 consent. Unbuilt wonders are deleted after 90 days.
- The tile shows only when nothing time-bound is waiting. A wonder lesson fills a dot but never proves anything.
- Voluntary sittings are the free-choice motivation measure (Deci 1999).

  5. A mistake worth talking about, with a line to say: "She used the whole-number rule on fractions. That's what learning looks like."
  6. Coming up in the next 14 days: check openings, reviews, school dates and prep days.
  7. One decision, if one is needed ("Keep 4 days?"), the friction question (none / some / a lot), and this week's one suggested conversation unless a nudge is waiting.
- An empty week is still sent, without shame: "No sittings this week. Monday's plan is ready: 10 minutes of fractions."
- The first three notes each carry one help-without-taking-over script: ask what they tried · offer the tutor's hint, not the answer · name the feeling.
- When mixed sets start, one line: "Mixed sets feel harder and score lower that day; that is how they work."
- At most 3 touches a week in total.
- Evidence: Bergman 2021 (+0.23 GPA); Bergman & Chan 2021 (course failures −27%); Kraft & Rogers 2015 (−41%; what-to-improve messages beat praise-only); Cortes 2021 (3 a week beat 1; 5 added nothing); Vasquez 2016.

**Rituals, each logged as a teaching act with an intent.**
- **Teach-back:** once a week by default, or twice if the grown-up allows, after a passed check or a finished lesson.
  - The grown-up gets two template questions and "You don't need to know the answer — just listen and ask", then taps "They showed me".
  - The tap is never proof, and it is dropped if it doesn't move first-review survival (Rittle-Johnson, Saylor & Swygert 2008; Kobayashi 2019).
- **K–2 dinner card:** weekly, with the answer and the exact words printed on it (Berkowitz 2015: the largest gains were for children of math-anxious parents).
- **"Our week":** about 3 minutes, opened from the Family card through `Handover`. Three screens, led by the child:
  1. **Show** one proof, replayed on the stage.
  2. **Next week**: target, anchor and rest days. Grades 6–9 write wish, obstacle and if-then, one line each.
  3. **Coming up** over the next 14 days.

  If it is skipped, last week's plan carries over silently. The landing page makes no achievement claim for it (PACT, Mayer 2019; Duckworth 2011; Musick & Meier 2012).
- **Rewards paragraph for parents:** if you reward, reward practice days, and keep it small, occasional and unexpected. Never reward scores. Never reward through the app; the proof record can't be bought (Deci 1999: expected rewards for taking part cost the most motivation, d = −.40, and unexpected ones cost none; Fryer 2011: paying for inputs, not scores, raised achievement).

**When no grown-up shows up.** The child's product is complete with zero adult action after setup, and nothing on the child's screen shows that an adult is missing. Weekly-review rates are tracked by language and household type.

**Guardians.** The account owner controls the guardian list, and removing someone takes effect immediately. A guardian who must not see the record never gets a note. Talk always shows the child **"Your grown-up can read these chats"** (ICO Std 11; Stattin & Kerr 2000).

**Refused, and why:**
- **Live camera, webcam proctoring and room scans:** proctoring is biased (Yoder-Himes 2022), and face geometry is biometric data under COPPA 2025.
- **Face or eye tracking:** invalid as an attention signal (Hutt 2019: gaze mind-wandering F1 ≈ .59), and biometric under COPPA 2025.
- **EEG, wearables and heart rate:** no learning evidence (fails b), and a heavy consent burden.
- **Emotion, attention or mood inference, including from voice:** the signals are invalid (Barrett 2019), and emotion recognition is banned in EU education (AI Act Art. 5(1)(f)).
- **Voiceprint or face login to tell siblings apart:** biometric under COPPA 2025; the profile picker does the job.
- **AR in 1.0:** scope, not harm.
- **Open hands-free chat as the K–2 floor:** child speech recognition misses about 25% of words (Attia 2024).

#### H. Rules that keep it honest and legal

**Honest:**
1. Practice is never proof, and the law's numbers are unchanged. Only proved skills are achievements.
2. Rapid answers are evidence in neither direction: no step down, no dot, no failed check, only a void.
3. Every number a grown-up sees comes from code. In-session accuracy and same-day mixed-set scores never become a parent-facing label.
4. Dots log days without scoring them, and tiny days are shown as tiny to the grown-up.
5. "They showed me", "a grown-up was nearby", sure / not sure and photo-checked work are stored as exactly that, never as proof.
6. Profile facts are labelled "trying" until decided. There are no learning-style labels (Pashler 2008).
7. The landing page claims no effect until the pilot's outside benchmark shows one. The week is never sold as the efficacy engine.
8. `i18n/en.ts:1681` (`land.parents.ask.body`: "There are no streaks, points or reminders for your child") and its ES twin are rewritten to describe the week honestly. Suggested: "A weekly rhythm you and your child set, with rest days built in. Nothing to lose, nothing to buy, and the app never messages your child." Circles are added to the landing only when they ship.

**Legal:**
1. **Under 13:** no child-to-child sharing outside the family account until all four parts of D's gate hold. The consent flow gains a `circle` scope; `lib/server/db/policy.ts` `CONSENT_SCOPES` is `["ai", "voice"]` today.
2. **Ages 13–15:** buddy and circle need every family's grown-up to approve. Before they ship, counsel confirms that a service whose main audience is under 13 may treat its 13–15 learners outside COPPA, and checks the Nebraska and Vermont codes. Age comes from the birthdate the grown-up entered, and a circle with an under-13 member follows the under-13 rules.
3. **Nothing is pushed by the app to a child.** A 13+ learner may put their own anchor on their own calendar. Messages to grown-ups: one-tap off, at most 3 touches a week, nothing between 21:00 and 07:00, and texts only to guardians who opted in.
4. **Ship opt-in until counsel signs off:** the default-on note (as a service message), the 13+ summaries-only view and the teacher share link.
5. **A written retention schedule** covers attempts, ink, photos (portfolio is opt-in, 2 years), wonders (unbuilt 90 days), circle data (term), transcripts and, in 1.1, human-tutor session recordings. Also required: a written infosec program, and separate consent for any non-integral third-party use. Vendor terms forbid retention and training.
6. **Voice** is streamed, transcribed and deleted. Only the transcript is kept, and there are no voiceprints.
7. **The teaching profile** is used only to teach (FTC 2022 edtech statement; Edmodo 2023). The Nebraska (2026) and Vermont (2027) design codes are added to the §5 counsel list.

### 3.4 The loop in the engine

**Mastery-law changes need the owner's sign-off (AGENTS rule 9).** The RULES numbers stay the same. The new rules:
- `rapidShare: 1/3`, `rapidFloorS: 1.5` and `rapidVoid: 2`: rapid answers are excluded from step-down, and checks are voided as in B.
- `retryNeedsNewAct`.
- `parkAfter: 2` more stuck sets and `parkDays: 10`, with a new `SkillState "parked"`.
- A planned worked example counts as help for `helpQuietMs`. It costs nothing, because examples sit far below the top level.
- `Attempt.accommodations` don't set `assisted`, except on skills flagged as measuring reading.
- `placementNext` may walk down to 3 grades below the enrolled grade, until two right answers in a row. Today it ends after two misses near one grade below (Muralidharan 2019, +0.37 SD).
- After 3 idle days, start one level lower.
- `readyOwn 9/10` and `checkPass 4/5` are **measured, not changed**, against a first-review survival band of 75–90%. Any change happens only with the owner (Rohrer 2005; Wilson 2019).

**3.2 Learner model.**
- `PROFILE_RULES.leadEach` goes from 3 to 12, and `reprEach` from 5 to 12.
- While a fact is undecided, the options alternate at random for that child (never in checks). The fact shows as "trying" and is re-checked monthly.
- New facts: anchor fit (share of sittings within 90 minutes of the anchor, by weekday), tiny-sitting share, finished vs abandoned by length, and right-on-own by input mode.
- All of these are suggestions to the grown-up only, and best time never falls within 2 hours of bedtime (Pashler 2008).

**3.3 Improvement loop.**
- Every teaching act is tagged with its **loop stage**: example, guided, retrieval, explain, check, review, wonder, teachback, weekreview. "Is it working?" then names the failing stage ("Recall is holding: 8 of 9 reviews passed. Guided practice is the weak spot; the tutor leads with examples this week").
- New `ActKind` / `Intent` pairs:
  - `teachback` / `retained`: the next review on that skill is right on own;
  - `weekreview` / `week-meets-target`;
  - `card` / `parent-acts`;
  - `why` (practice only);
  - `return`: the first set after a gap finishes;
  - `circle` / `week-meets-target`, logged weekly per member, so the loop can see whether circles move showing up.
- Metrics:
  - days a week vs target;
  - returns without prompting;
  - **voluntary sittings** (nothing assigned);
  - Done taps vs abandonment;
  - **first-review survival** at 7 and 21 days, target band 75–90%;
  - circle members' returns without prompting against their own 4 weeks before joining and against matched non-members.
- Guardrails:
  - child use after 21:00 = 0;
  - sittings over budget;
  - friction "a lot" in under 10% of weeks;
  - tiny share;
  - the circle leave rate and friction among circle families.
- Pilot method:
  - write expected effects before the first family, since experts overestimate effects by 30× or more (Zearn megastudy 2025);
  - 10 families show direction and harms only;
  - an outside benchmark;
  - a follow-up in weeks 8–12;
  - E2's three tiers and their citations live in the pilot protocol. A proposal to move a mechanic between tiers names its evidence for the age band, its smallest form and its kill rule.

**§6 additions.**
- Tutor evals: no empty praise; acknowledgements name a strategy; zero pining, guilt, "don't lose" or social-pressure lines (the week, the circle or a buddy is never given as a reason to practise).
- Natural use: required parent time ≤ 2 minutes a week; the product never asks for more than 5.
- e2e:
  - E2's Never list and D's copy rules hold on child surfaces in EN and ES;
  - no notification-permission request in a child session;
  - no group path reaches an under-13 child without `circle` consent.

### 3.2 The learner model — verified education and how to teach this child

Two halves, both computed from the record, nothing asked that can be derived.

**Verified education** (what they know, honestly):
- Skills proved under the mastery law, with dates and the standard behind each; skills practicing and
  their level; checks waiting; refreshes due.
- Courses finished and lessons done, with the check tally (own / helped / missed).
- School results, labelled "from school", never mixed with proof.
- Reading log. Together these are the transcript the Records page prints.

**Teaching profile** (what works for this child), each fact with the evidence count behind it:

| Fact | Derived from | Used by |
|---|---|---|
| Which hint rung usually unlocks them (nudge / strategy / first step) | hint uses followed by a right answer in the same set | tutor (`next_hint` starts there), item hints shown |
| Worked example vs hint: which leads to a right answer more often | "show me how" / `similar_problem` uses → next attempts | tutor leads with the one that works |
| Representation that lands (pictures, number line, blocks, words) | miss rates by visual kind at level 1; "explain differently" requests | course builder picks visuals; tutor's `show_visual` choice |
| Pace vs standard time | seconds per item vs `seconds` | set size; never a timer |
| Session length tolerance | sets abandoned vs finished by length and minute | planner's daily budget suggestion to the parent |
| Recurring misconceptions | `why` tags on chosen distractors across sets | tutor names the exact mistake; planner inserts a targeted set |
| Best time of day / weekday | right-on-own rate by hour and day | planner's suggestion to the parent, never a nudge to the child |
| Language and voice | locale; read-aloud and mic use | tutor band and modality |
| Interests | parent-set | examples |

Rules: derived from first-party learning data only; visible to the parent on the child's page as
**"How we teach {name}"** with the evidence ("3 of 4 times the first step did it"), editable
("prefer pictures", "no timer"), deletable; used only to teach; never for engagement, pricing or
anything shown to third parties. Under-13 profiling is a counsel item (§5); the design keeps it to
pedagogy and transparency so it can be defended as such.

### 3.3 The improvement loop — is what we intended happening?

Every teaching act writes a **teaching act** row: `{ learner, at, act, intent, context, outcome, at_outcome }`.

| Act | Intent | Outcome measured |
|---|---|---|
| hint (rung n) | the next try is right | right within the set? |
| worked example / similar problem | the learner finishes their own | right within the set? |
| practice set | the skill moves toward ready | level change; ready within N sets |
| check | prove or send back | passed / failed |
| prep set before a test | the test goes well | linked skills proved; school result (labelled) when the parent enters it |
| lesson | the lesson's check passes | tally |
| tutor conversation | the next attempts on that skill improve | right-on-own rate before vs after |
| source-built / AI course | finished and its checks passed | completion; tally; thumbs |
| plan line | done today | done / rolled |
| nudge to a parent | acted on | the suggested action happened |
| family goal (help now / daily / homeschool / organized) | the thing the goal names happens | unstuck same day; sets per week; records used; tests prepped |

**For this learner:** the teaching profile updates from these outcomes; the tutor and planner adapt
within the law (hint order, representation, set size, review spacing); the child page shows
**"Is it working?"** in plain words (e.g. "Hints are landing less than they did; the tutor will lead
with worked examples this week").

**For everyone (de-identified aggregates, thresholds ≥ 20 learners, never transcripts):**
- Items: miss rate by item seed-range and distractor; distractors nobody picks; hint rungs that never
  help; items missed by learners who had proved the prerequisites → a **content queue** in `/review`.
- Skills: where many get stuck → a missing prerequisite edge or a level split; proposed edits to the
  skill map, reviewed by a person before shipping.
- Tutor: conversations that did not improve the next attempts become eval cases (de-identified,
  consent-gated) so prompt changes are measured against real failures.
- Product: the four goals vs their outcomes, by band — the honest answer to "is it working" for the
  owner, on an internal page.
All aggregation runs server-side on the record; nothing is sold or shared; families can opt out of
aggregate improvement without losing anything.

## 6. Quality bars (what "good" means, measurably)

- **Correctness:** every computed skill verified by an independent route over ≥ 200 seeds per level,
  both languages; mutation checks on every strand; no draft bank ships as reviewed without a reviewer.
- **Tutor:** eval set of 40 scripted conversations; ≥ 90% deterministic pass (no answer before a try,
  ≤ 2 sentences + 1 question, no praise words, tools used for arithmetic, right language); median
  latency to first token < 1.5 s; cost per turn logged.
- **Voice:** first audio < 1.5 s after a reply starts; barge-in stops speech < 300 ms; a child's "mhm"
  does not end the tutor's sentence.
- **Honesty:** no number on any parent surface comes from a model; every AI-made thing is labelled;
  every fact has a source link (tests assert the label and the link).
- **Accessibility:** axe serious/critical = 0 on every screen at 1440 and 390; keyboard parity; 44 px
  targets (56 px for K–2 primary); reduced-motion equivalents; 320 px without overflow.
- **Performance:** Today interactive < 2 s on a mid phone; a practice set opens < 1 s; lesson stage
  first scene < 2 s.
- **Privacy:** no third-party scripts; knowledge calls carry only the query; names never leave the
  device except to our own server.
- **Tests:** unit on every pure module; e2e journeys for the four jobs at both sizes; evals with AI.
- **Improvement loop:** every hint, example, set, check and plan line records intent and outcome; the
  child page states "is it working" from those rows; the content queue flags items from aggregates;
  the teaching profile is visible, evidenced, editable and deletable.
- **Natural use (pilot):** ≥ 60% of learners return without being told to; parents spend < 5 minutes a
  week in the app unless they choose more; no child-facing reminder exists anywhere in the code.

## 9. Can I make something good and final?

Yes, with two honest qualifications.

- **The software and the content I can make.** Every mechanism in §3 is either built or a known build;
  the content targets in §4 are authoring work I can run in parallel with independent verification.
  "Final" for 1.0 is defined by §6 and the exit criteria below, not by a feeling.
- **Four things only people can supply:** a provider key and accounts (so the tutor and voice are real),
  counsel (so children can use it legally), a teacher and a Spanish reviewer (so the banks are not

  with them, the first version will be wrong about things only a six-year-old can show us, and the
  plan budgets two weeks to fix exactly that.

off · 10 families used it ≥ 3 weeks with ≥ 60% of learners returning without prompting · ≥ 7 of 10
families would keep it over what they used before for two of the four jobs (§1.2) · kaizenedu.net
serves this repo.
