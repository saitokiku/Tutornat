# Parent Research Kit — adults only, draft, nothing sent

Status: DRAFT by VP of Marketing (AI role). Nothing in this kit has been run with a participant. No recruitment message has been sent. No real child or family data may be collected with it.

## 0. Boundaries (binding)
- Participants are adults (parents/guardians, or adults who have been responsible for a K–8 child's schoolwork). No children participate, observe or are described by name.
- The demo is the saved synthetic candidate (redesign/candidate/index.html, offline, in-memory, made-up data). It is shown as "an early prototype," never as a product.
- No recording unless the owner decides; default is typed notes with a participant code (P1, P2…). No names, emails, school names or child names in any file under company/marketing/.
- No compensation is promised (budget unspecified). No outreach, posting or ads. The recruitment draft below is UNSENT until the owner answers POSITIONING.md Q2.
- Known demo limits to tell the facilitator, not the participant up front: mobile (320px) shows a stale learner status over content; use desktop. Help is scripted, not AI. Coordinator shell replay passed 30/30; full connected flow is not confirmed — expect to hit an edge.

## 1. Five neutral adult interview questions (20 minutes, before the demo)
Ask about past behavior, not opinions about the product. Do not describe the product before these are done.

1. "Think about the last full school week. How did you find out what your child had to do, and how did you keep track of it as the week went on?"
2. "Tell me about the most recent time your child got stuck on schoolwork. Walk me through it — from how you found out to how it ended."
3. "When you look at your child's finished work, what do you look for, and what do you usually do with what you see?"
4. "What do you use right now for school things at home — paper, texts, photos, school apps, anything — and what have you tried and stopped using? Why?"
5. "If one thing about handling schoolwork at home changed, what would you want it to be first? Have you already tried to change it?"

Optional language probe for bilingual households (only if relevant; do not lead): "Which language do you use for school things at home, and does it depend on who you're talking to?"

Facilitator rules: silence is fine; follow with "What happened next?" or "Can you give me an example?"; never say "would you use…" or "do you like…"; never mention AI, outcomes, or less chasing.

## 2. Task-based demo session (25 minutes, think-aloud, desktop)
Setup: candidate open in a browser on a laptop, synthetic family loaded, language set to the participant's preferred language, facilitator says: "This is an early prototype with made-up example data. Please think out loud. There are no wrong moves — if something is confusing, that is what we want to learn."

Tasks (give one at a time, as written; do not demonstrate first):
- T1 — Orientation: "Find what is due this week and tell me which item you'd deal with first." (observe DUE vs WORK vs SUGGESTED reading)
- T2 — Organize: "Add a piece of schoolwork your child might really have this week." (observe whether they reach for a real task; note what fields they expect)
- T3 — Child's side: "Now pretend you are your child. Start that task, ask for help, and mark that you're stuck." (observe whether 'ask for help' is understood as a request, not an answer)
- T4 — Review: "Back as yourself: find what your child did and leave the note you'd actually leave." (observe whether the note references the recorded work; capture the exact note text)
- T5 — Language: "Switch the app to the other language. Is your note still exactly as you wrote it? What changed and what didn't?" (observe verbatim preservation, reaction to localized labels)
- T6 — Closing: "Where would you look to see what happened this week?" (Activity / record)

Closing questions (5 minutes): "What did this do that you can't do today?" "What did it claim or seem to claim that you don't believe?" "What would you need to see before letting your child use the child side alone?" "Anything that felt fake, salesy, or like a template?"

## 4. Observable adoption and usability signals (what "promising" would look like)
These are signals to count, not claims to publish. Suggested thresholds are hypotheses for a first group of 5.
- Completes T1 and T4 without facilitator help (target: 4 of 5).
- Reads DUE, WORK and SUGGESTED as three different things without being told (target: 3 of 5).
- The T4 note references something specific in the recorded work rather than "good job" (target: 3 of 5).
- Brings a real current task into T2 unprompted (signal of a felt problem).
- Says, unprompted, that this would replace a specific current behavior (texts, asking, checking the portal).
- Asks whether it exists yet, or how to get it (strong; log the exact words).
- Spanish-speaking participants switch language in T5 and keep going without stumbling (weak evidence; still needs native review).

## 5. Disconfirmation criteria (what would tell us to change course)
Any one of these in a group of 5 is a stop-and-rethink signal; two or more means the positioning as written is wrong.
- 3 or more cannot describe a current tracking problem in Q1–Q5 (they are not chasing anything).
- 3 or more say their school's portal/app already covers what they need and the home record is not worth the effort.
- 3 or more treat "done" as "learned" despite the screens — the completion/mastery boundary is not landing and copy or UI must change before any claim.
- 3 or more expect "ask for help" to produce an answer (they read it as a tutor), i.e. the product reads as an AI tutor it is not.
- 3 or more would not let a child use the child side independently at the band they described.
- Any Spanish-speaking participant calls the draft Spanish wrong, unnatural or machine-like → Spanish is gated to native review before any further display.
- Any participant calls the screens "AI slop," "template," or "fake" about the core pages → owner review before more sessions.
- Nobody in the group references seeing the actual work as valuable → the "shared recorded work" promise is not a differentiator for this segment.
